2.1b Transmission Modes: Direction and Method
Simplex, half-duplex, full-duplex — and serial vs. parallel — the two independent choices behind every data transmission.
Key Terms
Simplex
Data flows in ONE direction only, e.g. computer to printer.
Half-duplex
Data flows both ways, but NOT at the same time, e.g. a walkie-talkie.
Full-duplex
Data flows both ways AT THE SAME TIME, e.g. a broadband connection.
Serial transmission
Data sent one bit at a time, over a single wire/channel.
Parallel transmission
Several bits (usually a byte) sent simultaneously, each down its own wire.
Skewing
Bits arriving out of synchronisation — a risk unique to parallel transmission over long distances.
Theory

Three factors in any data transmission

  • Direction — can data flow one way only, or both ways?
  • Method — how many bits are sent at the same time?
  • Synchronisation — how is the received data kept in the correct order?
  • These factors are normally governed by a communication protocol.

Direction: simplex, half-duplex, full-duplex

  • Simplex: data travels in ONE direction only (e.g. sender → receiver). Example: sending data from a computer to a printer.
  • Half-duplex: data can travel BOTH directions along the same line, but not simultaneously. Example: a walkie-talkie — you can send or receive, never both at once.
  • Full-duplex: data travels BOTH directions AT THE SAME TIME along the same line. Example: a broadband internet connection.
The three transmission directions — one-way only, alternating, and simultaneous both ways.

Method: serial vs. parallel

  • Serial transmission: data sent ONE BIT AT A TIME over a SINGLE wire/channel, one after another as a stream. Can be simplex, half-duplex, or full-duplex.
  • Serial works well over LONG distances, though it's slower than parallel. Because only one channel is used, data arrives fully synchronised (in the correct order) — no risk of skewing. Example: USB.
  • Parallel transmission: SEVERAL bits (usually a byte) sent down SEVERAL wires/channels simultaneously — one bit per wire. Can also be simplex, half-duplex, or full-duplex.
  • Parallel works well over SHORT distances only. Beyond about 20 metres, bits can become skewed (arrive out of sync) — the longer the wire, the worse this gets. It's faster than serial, which is why computers use it internally between components (short distances, high speed needed).

Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — transmission direction (simplex/half/full-duplex) and method (serial/parallel).

Short answer 1
Give a real-world example of simplex, half-duplex, and full-duplex transmission (one each).
Reveal answer

Simplex: computer to printer. Half-duplex: a walkie-talkie. Full-duplex: a broadband internet connection.

Short answer 2
Why does serial transmission never suffer from skewing, while parallel transmission can?
Reveal answer

Serial uses only ONE wire/channel, so bits travel one after another in a single stream and can't get out of order relative to each other. Parallel uses MULTIPLE wires simultaneously, and over long distances the signals on different wires can arrive at slightly different times, causing bits to become desynchronised (skewed).

Fill in the blank 3
Data being sent 8 bits at a time in one direction only is: ______ (direction) + ______ (method) transmission.
Reveal answer

Blank 1: simplex. Blank 2: parallel.

Spot the error 4
A student writes: "Parallel transmission is always the better choice since it's faster than serial." What real trade-off does this claim ignore?
Reveal answer

It ignores that parallel only works well over SHORT distances — beyond around 20 metres, bits risk arriving skewed (out of sync), and the problem gets worse the longer the wire. Serial, while slower, is more reliable over long distances precisely because it avoids this risk entirely.

Applied / scenario 5
A computer's internal circuit board needs to move data between components at very high speed, over distances of only a few centimetres. Which transmission method is more appropriate — serial or parallel — and why, using this lesson's content?
Reveal answer

Parallel. The lesson states that parallel is faster than serial and works well over short distances, and that internal computer circuits specifically use parallel transmission for exactly this reason — the distance is short enough to avoid the skewing risk, while the higher speed is genuinely needed.

Short answer 6
What are the three factors that must be considered whenever data is transmitted?
Reveal answer

The direction of transmission, the method of transmission (how many bits at once), and how the data will be synchronised.
