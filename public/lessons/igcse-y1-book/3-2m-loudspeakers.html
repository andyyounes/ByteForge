3.2m Output Devices: Loudspeakers
From stored binary data to a vibrating cone — DACs, amplifiers, and electromagnets.
Key Terms
DAC
Digital-to-Analogue Converter — turns stored binary sound data into an electric current.
Amplifier
Boosts the small current from the DAC into a current strong enough to drive a loudspeaker.
Electromagnet
A temporary magnet created when current flows through a coil wrapped around an iron core.
Theory

From digital file to electric current

  • Digitised sound stored in a file must be converted back to sound: first, the digital data passes through a DAC (Digital-to-Analogue Converter), turning it into an electric current.
  • This current is very small, so it's passed through an amplifier, boosting it to a level strong enough to actually drive a loudspeaker.
  • The amplified current is finally fed to the loudspeaker, where it's converted into sound.
Digital data → DAC (converts to small analogue current) → amplifier (boosts it) → loudspeaker.

How the loudspeaker turns current into sound

  • The electric current flows through a coil of wire wrapped around an IRON CORE, temporarily turning the core into an electromagnet.
  • A PERMANENT MAGNET sits close to this electromagnet. As the current VARIES, the induced magnetic field also varies, causing the iron core to be attracted towards/away from the permanent magnet — making it VIBRATE.
  • The iron core is attached to a CONE (paper or thin synthetic material). As the core vibrates, the cone vibrates too — producing the actual sound waves we hear.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — loudspeakers.

Short answer 1
What is the role of the DAC in playing digitally stored sound through a loudspeaker?
Reveal answer

The DAC (Digital-to-Analogue Converter) converts the digital (binary) sound data into an electric current — an analogue signal the rest of the audio chain can work with.

Short answer 2
Why is an amplifier needed between the DAC and the loudspeaker?
Reveal answer

The current produced directly by the DAC is very small — too weak to drive a loudspeaker on its own. The amplifier boosts it to a level strong enough to actually power the speaker.

Fill in the blank 3
Inside the loudspeaker, current flowing through a coil wrapped around an iron core turns it into a temporary ______, which vibrates against a ______ magnet.
Reveal answer

Blank 1: electromagnet. Blank 2: permanent.

Spot the error 4
A student claims: "The cone in a loudspeaker vibrates because the DAC sends it electric current directly." What real step does this skip?
Reveal answer

This skips the AMPLIFIER — the DAC's current must first be amplified before it's fed to the loudspeaker's coil. It also skips the actual mechanism inside the speaker: the current makes the iron core an electromagnet, which vibrates against a permanent magnet, and THAT vibration (via the attached cone) is what produces sound — not the current acting on the cone directly.

Applied / scenario 5
Describe, step by step, the full journey of a digital music file stored on a computer's hard drive, from storage to audible sound coming out of a connected loudspeaker.
Reveal answer

1. The digital (binary) music data is read from storage and passed through a DAC, converting it into a small analogue electric current.

2. This current passes through an amplifier, boosting it to a level strong enough to drive the loudspeaker.

3. The amplified current flows through the loudspeaker's coil, temporarily turning the iron core into an electromagnet.

4. As the current varies, the core vibrates against the nearby permanent magnet, and this vibration is transferred to the attached cone, producing sound waves.

Short answer 6
What material is the loudspeaker's cone typically made from?
Reveal answer

Paper or a thin synthetic material.
