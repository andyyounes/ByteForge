3.2f Touch Screen Technologies
Capacitive, infrared, and resistive — three genuinely different ways a screen detects a touch.
Key Terms
Capacitive touch screen
Detects touch via a change in an electrostatic field, since human skin conducts electricity.
Infrared touch screen
Detects touch when a finger breaks an infrared beam crossing the screen surface.
Resistive touch screen
Detects touch when pressure makes two resistive layers make contact, completing a circuit.
Multi-touch
Detecting more than one simultaneous touch point (e.g. pinch/zoom gestures).
Theory

Capacitive touch screens

  • Structure: a protective glass layer, a transparent conductive electrode layer, and a glass substrate.
  • Since human skin conducts electricity, a bare finger (or special stylus) touching the screen changes the conductive layer's ELECTROSTATIC FIELD. A microcontroller calculates where this change occurred to find the touch coordinates.
  • Surface capacitive: sensors at the corners with small applied voltages create an electric field; a touch draws current from each corner, reducing capacitance — only works with bare finger/stylus.
  • Projective capacitive: the conductive layer forms an X-Y matrix, creating a 3D electrostatic field. A touch disturbs this field. Works with bare fingers, stylus, AND thin gloves — and supports MULTI-TOUCH (e.g. pinch/slide).

Infrared touch screens

  • A glass screen is surrounded by an array of infrared TRANSMITTERS (in a matrix across the screen) and infrared SENSORS (around the edges).
  • Touching the screen breaks one or more infrared beams, reducing the infrared radiation reaching the corresponding sensors.
  • A microcontroller uses this reduction pattern to calculate exactly where the screen was touched.

Resistive touch screens

  • Two layers of electrically resistive material with a voltage across them: an upper FLEXIBLE polyethylene layer (resistive coating on one side) and a bottom GLASS layer (usually indium tin oxide coating). Separated by air or an inert gas (e.g. argon).
  • Touching the top surface presses the two layers together, completing a circuit — a flow of electricity occurs, and the touch point is found from the resulting CHANGE IN VOLTAGE.
  • A microcontroller converts this voltage change into digital data, sent to the microprocessor.
Comparing the three touch screen technologies
TechnologyAdvantagesDisadvantages
CapacitiveBetter clarity than resistive (esp. in sunlight); very durable/scratch-resistant; projective type allows multi-touchSurface type needs bare finger/stylus only; sensitive to electromagnetic radiation

Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — capacitive, infrared, and resistive touch screens.

Short answer 1
Why does a capacitive touch screen work with human skin, but generally not with an ordinary (non-conductive) glove?
Reveal answer

Capacitive screens detect touch by a change in an ELECTROSTATIC FIELD, which relies on the touching object being a conductor of electricity. Human skin conducts electricity; an ordinary glove doesn't, so it can't create the same field change (though special thin conductive gloves DO work with projective capacitive screens).

Short answer 2
How does an infrared touch screen detect where it has been touched?
Reveal answer

An array of infrared transmitters/sensors surrounds the screen. Touching the screen breaks one or more infrared beams, reducing the radiation reaching the corresponding sensors — a microcontroller uses this pattern to calculate the touch position.

Fill in the blank 3
A resistive touch screen has two ______ layers separated by ______; touching the top surface presses them together, completing a circuit.
Reveal answer

Blank 1: resistive. Blank 2: air (or an inert gas, e.g. argon).

Spot the error 4
A student claims: "All three touch screen technologies support multi-touch, since they're all modern touch screens." What's wrong with this?
Reveal answer

Resistive touch screens explicitly do NOT support multi-touch — this is listed as one of their disadvantages. Only projective capacitive and infrared touch screens support multi-touch according to this lesson.

Applied / scenario 5
An outdoor kiosk needs a touch screen that will be used in bright sunlight, occasionally gets rained on, and must be usable by people wearing thin gloves in cold weather. Which touch screen technology best fits ALL these requirements, and why, using this lesson's content?
Reveal answer

Projective capacitive. It offers better clarity than resistive especially in strong sunlight, and the lesson states projective capacitive screens work with thin surgical or cotton gloves as well as bare fingers. (Resistive resists water well but has poor visibility in sunlight; infrared is sensitive to water/moisture, ruling it out for occasional rain exposure.)

Short answer 6
Name one real drawback shared by BOTH infrared and capacitive touch screens, based on this lesson.
Reveal answer

Sensitivity to electromagnetic/environmental interference — capacitive screens are sensitive to electromagnetic radiation, and infrared screens are sensitive to light interference and can accidentally activate if the beams are disturbed.
