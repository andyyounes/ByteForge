3.2g Output Devices: Actuators & Light Projectors
From an electrical signal to physical motion, and from millions of tiny mirrors to a full-colour image on a wall.
Key Terms
Actuator
A mechanical/electromechanical device (relay, solenoid, motor) that converts an electrical signal into physical motion.
Solenoid
A coil that converts an electrical signal into a magnetic field, producing linear (or rotary) motion of a plunger.
DLP projector
Digital Light Projector — uses millions of micro-mirrors on a DMD chip to form an image.
LCD projector
Uses dichroic mirrors and three LCD screens (red/green/blue) recombined via a prism.
Theory

Actuators

  • An actuator is needed whenever a computer must control a physical device — e.g. starting/stopping a conveyor belt, or opening/closing a valve.
  • A solenoid converts an electrical signal into a magnetic field, producing LINEAR motion: a magnetised plunger inside the coil moves when current is applied.
  • Rotary solenoids use a cylindrical coil — applying current causes ROTATIONAL movement of the plunger instead.
A solenoid: current through the coil creates a magnetic field that moves the plunger — this is how an actuator turns "electrical" into "physical".

Digital Light Projectors (DLP)

  • Key component: a DMD chip (Digital Micromirror Device) with MILLIONS of tiny mirrors. The number/arrangement of mirrors determines image resolution.
  • Each mirror is either ON (tilted TOWARDS the light source) or OFF (tilted AWAY) — creating a light or dark pixel.
  • Mirrors switch thousands of times per second, producing greyscale shades (typically 1,024 grey shades) — more "on" time = lighter grey.
  • White light passes through a colour filter, splitting it into red/green/blue. The DLP can produce over 16 million colours by linking each mirror's ON/OFF timing to the filtered colours.

LCD Projectors (older technology)

  • A powerful white light beam passes through dichroic (chromatic-coated) mirrors, reflecting back light at wavelengths corresponding to red, green, and blue.
  • Each of these 3 colour components passes through its OWN LCD screen (thousands of pixels that block or allow light) — producing 3 separate monochromatic images (one all-red, one all-green, one all-blue version of the same picture).
  • A special PRISM recombines these three images into one full-colour image, which then passes through the lens onto the screen.
DLP vs. LCD — the key structural difference

DLP uses ONE chip with millions of tiny mirrors, switching on/off to create both brightness AND colour. LCD uses THREE separate LCD screens (one per colour) whose outputs are recombined via a prism — a fundamentally different, older approach.

Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — actuators and light projectors.

Short answer 1
What does a solenoid actually convert, and what type of motion does a standard (non-rotary) solenoid produce?
Reveal answer

It converts an ELECTRICAL SIGNAL into a MAGNETIC FIELD, which produces LINEAR motion of the plunger inside the coil.

Short answer 2
In a DLP projector, what does it mean for a micro-mirror to be "ON" vs. "OFF"?
Reveal answer

"ON" means the mirror tilts TOWARDS the light source (creating a light pixel). "OFF" means it tilts AWAY from the light source (creating a dark pixel).

Fill in the blank 3
An LCD projector splits white light into red, green, and blue using ______ mirrors, then passes each colour through its own ______ screen.
Reveal answer

Blank 1: dichroic (chromatic-coated). Blank 2: LCD.

Spot the error 4
A student claims: "A DLP projector produces its colours using three separate coloured LCD screens, just like an LCD projector." What's wrong with this?
Reveal answer

This describes the LCD projector's method, not the DLP's. A DLP projector uses ONE DMD chip with millions of micro-mirrors, switching on/off in sync with a colour filter to produce colour — it does NOT use three separate LCD screens at all; that's specifically the older LCD projector's technique.

Applied / scenario 5
A factory control system needs to open a valve when a sensor detects a certain pressure level. Using this lesson's content, explain what type of component is needed to actually perform the physical action of opening the valve, and how it works.
Reveal answer

An actuator — specifically, a solenoid could be used. When the control system sends an electrical signal to the solenoid's coil, this creates a magnetic field that moves a magnetised plunger, producing the linear motion needed to physically open the valve.

Short answer 6
How does a DLP projector produce different shades of grey?
Reveal answer

By switching each micro-mirror on and off thousands of times per second — the RATIO of on-time to off-time determines the shade: a mirror on more often produces a lighter grey, and typically up to 1,024 distinct grey shades can be produced this way.
