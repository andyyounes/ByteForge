6.1d Automated Systems: Weather Stations & Gaming
A tipping bucket that measures rainfall on its own, and the sensors that make a game controller feel real.
Key Terms
Anemometer
Measures wind speed.
Hygrometer
Measures humidity.
Barometer
Measures air pressure.
Tipping bucket rain gauge
The one actuator-driven part of a weather station — tips a full bucket to measure rainfall.
Theory

Automated weather stations

  • Save labour, gather data from remote regions or wherever CONSTANT weather data is needed.
  • Needs: a microprocessor, storage (database), battery (often solar-charged), and a range of sensors:
  • Thermometer (temperature), anemometer (wind speed), hygrometer (humidity), barometer (air pressure), level sensor (rainfall), light sensor (hours of daylight).
  • All sensor data goes to the microprocessor, which performs calculations (e.g. daylight hours, actual rainfall, wind direction) and stores results in a central database.
  • Some stations near airports automatically send reports to nearby pilots every 5 MINUTES.
The ONE part that needs an actuator

The tipping bucket rain gauge is the only weather-station component using an actuator. At a set time interval, the microprocessor signals an actuator to operate a piston, tipping a full bucket of collected rainwater into a vessel. Level sensors then measure how much rain fell in that interval.

Gaming

  • Sensors add realism to games:
  • Accelerometers: measure acceleration/deceleration — respond to tilting the device forward/backward, side to side.
  • Proximity sensors: used in smart touch pads — embedded electrodes detect hand/finger position, increasing user awareness.
  • Combined with a microcontroller, these sensors let players' physical actions simulate real events, creating a more IMMERSIVE gaming experience.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — automated weather stations and gaming sensors.

Short answer 1
Which part of an automated weather station is the ONLY one that uses an actuator, and what does it do?
Reveal answer

The tipping bucket rain gauge. At a set interval, an actuator operates a piston that tips a full bucket of rainwater into a vessel, where level sensors measure how much rain fell.

Short answer 2
Match each instrument to what it measures: anemometer, hygrometer, barometer.
Reveal answer

Anemometer = wind speed. Hygrometer = humidity. Barometer = air pressure.

Fill in the blank 3
A gaming device's ______ measures tilting forward/backward and side to side; its ______ sensors detect finger/hand position on a touch pad.
Reveal answer

Blank 1: accelerometer. Blank 2: proximity.

Spot the error 4
A student claims: "Every sensor in an automated weather station is connected to its own dedicated actuator." What's wrong with this?
Reveal answer

Only ONE part — the tipping bucket rain gauge — actually uses an actuator. All the other sensors (thermometer, anemometer, hygrometer, barometer, light sensor) simply send readings to the microprocessor for processing/storage; they don't drive any actuator at all.

Applied / scenario 5
An airport uses a nearby automated weather station to keep incoming pilots updated on conditions. Using this lesson's content, describe how often this update happens, and why frequent updates matter for aviation.
Reveal answer

Reports are sent automatically every 5 minutes to pilots in the vicinity. Frequent updates matter because weather conditions (wind, pressure, visibility) can change quickly and are critical to safe landing/take-off decisions — a stale reading could be dangerously misleading.

Short answer 6
Why do gaming consoles combine accelerometers and proximity sensors with a microcontroller?
Reveal answer

To allow increased human interaction with the game — physical actions (tilting, touching) can simulate real events, giving a more immersive gaming experience.
