IGCSE Year 1 · Unit 5 · The Internet & Its Uses
5.3e Social Engineering
No hacking required — just fear, curiosity, or trust, exploited until the victim opens the door themselves.
Key Terms
Social engineering
Manipulating people into breaking normal security procedures by exploiting human emotion.
Baiting
Leaving an infected device (e.g. memory stick) somewhere to be found and plugged in out of curiosity.
Scareware
A fake pop-up claiming infection, tricking the user into downloading fake anti-virus (real malware).
Theory

What social engineering is

  • A cybercriminal creates a SOCIAL situation that makes the victim drop their guard — manipulating people into breaking normal security procedures.
  • Crucially: there is NO hacking involved — the user WILLINGLY allows access, downloads malware, or visits a fake site, having been rushed into a rash decision.
Instant messaging
Malicious links embedded in messages (e.g. "important software upgrade").
Relies on: curiosity
Emails/phishing scams
User tricked by an apparently genuine email, opens a link redirecting to a fake site.
Relies on: trust in well-known companies
Baiting
Infected memory stick left somewhere to be found; the finder plugs it in "just to see who it belongs to".
Relies on: curiosity
Phone calls
A fake "IT professional" claims the device is compromised, convincing the user to download software giving the attacker control.
Relies on: fear
Scareware
A pop-up falsely claims a virus infection, urging the user to download "fake anti-virus" immediately.
Relies on: fear

The three emotions exploited

  • Fear: the user is panicked into believing immediate danger exists, with no time to logically assess if it's real.
  • Curiosity: tricked into believing they've won something, or curious who a found device belongs to — curiosity overrides careful judgement.
  • Empathy and trust: a genuine belief that well-known, genuine-sounding companies can always be trusted — a dangerous assumption cybercriminals fully exploit.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — social engineering.

Short answer 1
Why is social engineering said to involve "no hacking"?
Reveal answer

Because the user WILLINGLY grants access, downloads the malicious software, or visits the fake website themselves — they are manipulated into making the rash decision, rather than the system being broken into against their will.

Short answer 2
What is "baiting", and which human emotion does it exploit?
Reveal answer

Baiting is leaving an infected device (e.g. a memory stick) somewhere to be found, hoping the finder plugs it in out of curiosity — it relies on CURIOSITY.

Fill in the blank 3
Scareware relies on the emotion of ______, while trusting a genuine-sounding company's email relies on ______ and trust.
Reveal answer

Blank 1: fear. Blank 2: empathy.

Spot the error 4
A student claims: "Social engineering attacks always involve technically breaking into a computer system, just like hacking does." What's wrong with this?
Reveal answer

The lesson explicitly states there is NO hacking involved in social engineering — the victim WILLINGLY grants access, downloads malware, or visits the fake site themselves, manipulated by emotion rather than any technical system breach.

Applied / scenario 5
An employee receives an urgent phone call from someone claiming to be from IT support, saying their computer has been compromised and they must download a "fix" immediately. Using this lesson's content, name this specific social engineering tactic, and the emotion it exploits.
Reveal answer

This is the phone calls tactic. It exploits fear — the urgency and claimed compromise are designed to panic the victim into acting immediately, without time to logically assess whether the call is genuine.

Short answer 6
Name the five social engineering tactics listed in this lesson.
Reveal answer

Instant messaging, emails/phishing scams, baiting, phone calls, and scareware.
