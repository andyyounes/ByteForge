3.3d Optical Media: CD, DVD & Blu-ray
Laser light, spiral tracks, and why a shorter wavelength means more storage.
Key Terms
Pits and lands
The two physical states along an optical disc's spiral track that laser light distinguishes to read data.
Dual-layering
Two separate recording layers joined with a transparent spacer, doubling potential storage on a DVD/Blu-ray.
R / RW
'R' discs can be written once only; 'RW' discs can be written to and read from many times.
Theory

How CDs and DVDs work

  • Both use a thin layer of metal alloy or light-sensitive organic dye, with data stored in pits and lands along a single SPIRAL track running from the centre to the edge.
  • As the disc spins, the optical head follows this spiral track outward. Like an HDD, the disc is divided into sectors for direct access — and (like an HDD) the OUTER part of the disc moves faster than the inner part.
  • A RED laser reads/writes the data. Discs are 'R' (write once) or 'RW' (read/write many times).
  • Dual-layering (DVD): two recording layers joined by a transparent polycarbonate spacer, with a thin reflector between them. The laser refocuses a fraction of a millimetre to read the second layer.
  • Standard single-layer DVDs still hold more data than CDs, because DVD pit size and track width are both SMALLER — DVDs use a 650nm laser wavelength vs. CD's 780nm. Shorter wavelength = greater storage capacity.
A single continuous SPIRAL track (not concentric rings like an HDD), running from the disc's centre outward.

Blu-ray — the next step

  • Named "Blu-ray" (not "Blue-ray") since "Blue" couldn't be copyrighted.
  • Uses a BLUE laser (405nm wavelength, vs. red's 650nm for DVD) — the shorter wavelength allows much smaller pits/lands, giving up to 5× more storage than a standard DVD.
  • Single-layer Blu-ray uses a 1.2mm polycarbonate disk; dual-layer Blu-ray (and DVDs) use a sandwich of two 0.6mm disks.
  • Blu-ray discs come with built-in ENCRYPTION to help prevent piracy/copyright infringement.
  • Data transfer rate: DVD = 10 Mbps; Blu-ray = 36 Mbps (25 GiB transfers in about 1.5 hours).

Blu-ray's extra interactivity

  • Record HD TV programmes; skip instantly to any part of the disc; create playlists of recordings; edit/re-order recorded programmes; automatically find empty space to avoid over-recording; access websites and download subtitles/extras.
  • All optical media (CD/DVD/Blu-ray) are used for backups (photos, music, multimedia) and file transfer; manufacturers often supply software (e.g. printer drivers) on read-only CDs/DVDs.
  • Movies/games mainly use DVD/Blu-ray since a CD's capacity is too small for most movies.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — CD, DVD, and Blu-ray optical media.

Short answer 1
Why does a shorter laser wavelength allow more data to be stored on an optical disc?
Reveal answer

A shorter wavelength laser can read/write much SMALLER pits and lands, so more of them can fit in the same physical space — this is exactly why Blu-ray (405nm) holds far more data than DVD (650nm), which in turn holds more than CD (780nm).

Short answer 2
What is the real reason the format is called "Blu-ray" rather than "Blue-ray"?
Reveal answer

It was impossible to copyright the word "Blue", so "Blu" was used instead.

Fill in the blank 3
A disc marked 'R' can be written to ______ time(s) only; a disc marked 'RW' can be written to and read from ______ times.
Reveal answer

Blank 1: one. Blank 2: many.

Spot the error 4
A student claims: "CDs and DVDs store data on tracks that are separate concentric rings, exactly like a hard disk drive." What's wrong with this?
Reveal answer

Optical discs use a single CONTINUOUS SPIRAL track running from the centre to the edge — not separate concentric rings like an HDD's platters. This is a genuinely different physical layout, even though both are divided into sectors for direct access.

Applied / scenario 5
A film studio wants to distribute a 4-hour high-definition movie on physical disc, and also wants built-in protection against piracy. Which format should they choose — DVD or Blu-ray — and why, based on this lesson's content?
Reveal answer

Blu-ray (dual-layer, 50GB, holding 4.5 hours of HD content — enough for the 4-hour movie). A standard dual-layer DVD (4.7GB, ~2 hours SD) genuinely cannot hold a 4-hour HD movie. Blu-ray also comes with built-in encryption to help prevent piracy, directly matching the studio's second requirement.

Short answer 6
Name two real interactivity features Blu-ray offers that DVD doesn't, according to this lesson.
Reveal answer

Any two of: recording HD TV programmes; instant skip to any disc part; creating playlists; editing/re-ordering recordings; auto-finding empty space; accessing websites/downloading subtitles or extras.
