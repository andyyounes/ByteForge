3.2j Output Devices: Laser Printers
Static electricity, a charged drum, and dry toner — how a laser printer produces a whole page at once.
Key Terms
Toner
Dry powdered ink used by laser printers, instead of the liquid ink used by inkjets.
Printing drum
A charged rotating cylinder that a laser scans to selectively remove charge, forming the page's text/image pattern.
Fuser
A set of heated rollers that melts toner onto the paper permanently.
Theory

What makes laser printers different

  • Laser printers use DRY POWDER toner instead of liquid ink, relying on STATIC ELECTRICITY.
  • Unlike inkjets (which print line by line), laser printers print the WHOLE PAGE in one go.
  • Colour laser printers use 4 toner cartridges: cyan, magenta, yellow, and black.
The laser printing sequence
  1. Document data sent to the printer driver
  2. Driver converts data into a format the printer understands
  3. Driver checks the printer is available (not busy/offline/out of toner)
  4. Data sent to printer, stored in the printer buffer
  5. The printing DRUM is given a positive charge; a laser scans across it, removing charge from certain areas — leaving NEGATIVELY charged areas matching the page's text/images
  6. The drum is coated with POSITIVELY charged toner — it only sticks to the negatively charged (image) areas
  7. A NEGATIVELY charged sheet of paper rolls over the drum
  8. The toner transfers from drum to paper, forming an exact copy of the page
  9. The paper's electric charge is removed (so it doesn't stick to the drum)
  10. The paper passes through the FUSER (heated rollers) — heat melts the toner so it fixes permanently
  11. A discharge lamp removes all remaining charge from the drum, resetting it for the next page
When to use a laser printer

High-volume printing (colour or monochrome), fast multiple copies, high-quality output — e.g. producing many flyers/posters for advertising. Advantages: large toner cartridges and large paper trays (often more than a ream).

When to use an inkjet printer

One-off photos, or a small number of good-quality colour pages, where smaller cartridges/trays aren't a limitation.

Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — laser printers.

Short answer 1
What key physical principle does a laser printer rely on that an inkjet printer does not?
Reveal answer

Static electricity — the drum, toner, and paper are all electrically charged, and the laser printer relies on opposite/matching charges to place toner precisely where needed.

Short answer 2
Why does toner only stick to certain parts of the drum, and not the whole thing?
Reveal answer

The toner is positively charged, and it only sticks to the NEGATIVELY charged areas of the drum — these negative areas are exactly where the laser removed the drum's original positive charge, matching the page's text/image pattern.

Fill in the blank pseudocode 3
After the toner transfers to the paper, the paper passes through the ______, where heated rollers melt the toner so it fixes ______ to the paper.
Reveal answer

Blank 1: fuser. Blank 2: permanently.

Spot the error 4
A student claims: "A laser printer prints a page one line at a time, moving the print head side to side, just like an inkjet printer." What's wrong with this?
Reveal answer

Laser printers print the WHOLE PAGE in one go, using a charged drum and toner — there's no moving print head spraying line by line. This line-by-line process describes an inkjet printer, not a laser printer.

Applied / scenario 5
A business needs to print 500 copies of a high-quality colour flyer as quickly as possible. Using this lesson's content, recommend inkjet or laser, and justify your choice.
Reveal answer

Laser. The lesson states laser printers are ideal for high-volume printing (colour or monochrome) and are very fast when making multiple copies, with large toner cartridges and large paper trays — a direct match for printing 500 flyers quickly.

Short answer 6
Why must the discharge lamp remove all remaining charge from the drum at the end of the cycle?
Reveal answer

To reset the drum back to a neutral state, ready to be given a fresh positive charge and scanned again for the NEXT page — without this reset, leftover charge patterns from the previous page could interfere with printing the next one.
