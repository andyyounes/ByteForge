5.2a Digital Currency & Cryptocurrency
Money that only exists as data — and why cryptocurrency ditched the central bank entirely.
Key Terms
Fiat currency
Conventional currency ($, £, €) backed by governments/banks, not gold/silver reserves.
Digital currency
Currency existing purely in digital form, relying on a central banking system.
Cryptocurrency
A decentralised digital currency using cryptography, with no central bank/government control.
Theory

What digital currency is

  • Exists PURELY in digital format — no physical form, unlike fiat currency ($, £, €, ¥).
  • "Fiat" is Latin for "let it be done" — fiat currency is backed by governments/banks, not linked to gold/silver reserves.
  • Digital currency is an accepted PAYMENT method, transferable between accounts like cash or cards — enabling online banking (e.g. PayPal) or phone apps (e.g. Apple Pay).
  • Money exists only as DATA on a computer system, but can be converted into physical cash if needed.
  • Digital currency relies on a CENTRAL banking system — e.g. sending money via Bank X and Bank Y both routes through a Central Bank.

The centralisation problem — and cryptocurrency's answer

  • Centralisation creates real CONFIDENTIALITY and SECURITY issues — a persistent problem for digital currency systems.
  • Cryptocurrency uses cryptography to track transactions, addressing these centralisation problems by being DECENTRALISED.
  • Traditional digital currencies are regulated by central banks/governments (transactions, exchange rates). Cryptocurrency has NO state control — rules are set by the cryptocurrency COMMUNITY itself.
  • Unlike traditional digital currency, cryptocurrency transactions are PUBLICLY available — trackable, with the total money supply monitored.
  • Cryptocurrency works within a blockchain network, making it MUCH more secure (covered in the next lesson).
Digital currency routes through banks and a central authority; cryptocurrency transacts directly, wallet-to-wallet.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — digital currency and cryptocurrency.

Short answer 1
Where does the term "fiat currency" come from, and what does it mean?
Reveal answer

It's Latin, meaning "let it be done" — fiat currency is backed by governments/banks rather than being linked to gold or silver reserves.

Short answer 2
What real problem with traditional digital currency did cryptocurrency set out to solve?
Reveal answer

The confidentiality and security problems caused by CENTRALISATION — traditional digital currency relies on a central banking system, which has ongoing security/confidentiality issues. Cryptocurrency solves this via decentralisation.

Fill in the blank 3
Traditional digital currency is regulated by ______ banks and governments; cryptocurrency has no ______ control — rules are set by its own community.
Reveal answer

Blank 1: central. Blank 2: state.

Spot the error 4
A student claims: "Cryptocurrency transactions are completely private and untrackable, unlike traditional digital currency." What's wrong with this?
Reveal answer

This is backwards — the lesson states cryptocurrency transactions are actually PUBLICLY available and CAN be tracked, with the total money in the system monitored. It's traditional digital currency's centralised, non-public records that keep transactions more hidden from the public.

Applied / scenario 5
A community wants a currency system with no single government or bank able to unilaterally control exchange rates or transaction rules. Using this lesson's content, explain why cryptocurrency, rather than traditional digital currency, matches this goal.
Reveal answer

Traditional digital currency is explicitly regulated by central banks and governments, who determine transactions and exchange rates. Cryptocurrency has NO state control at all — its rules are set entirely by the cryptocurrency community itself, directly matching the community's goal of avoiding single-authority control.

Short answer 6
Give two real examples of how digital currency is used in everyday life, according to this lesson.
Reveal answer

Online banking (e.g. PayPal), and smartphone payment apps (e.g. Apple Pay).
