3.2d Input Devices: The Optical Mouse
How a beam of red light, 1500 photos a second, turns hand movement into an on-screen cursor.
Key Terms
Optical mouse
A pointing device using a red LED and tiny camera to track movement on virtually any surface.
CMOS
Complementary Metal Oxide Semiconductor — picks up the reflected red light and generates electric pulses.
DSP
Digital Signal Processor — works out the mouse's coordinates from the changing image patterns.
Theory

How an optical mouse works

  • An optical mouse is a POINTING DEVICE using tiny cameras to take up to 1,500 images per second.
  • Unlike an older MECHANICAL mouse (which relies on a rolling ball), the optical mouse works on virtually ANY surface.
  • Process: a red LED in the base shines light onto the surface → the reflection is picked up by a CMOS sensor → the CMOS generates electric pulses representing the reflected light → these pulses go to a DSP (Digital Signal Processor) → the DSP works out the mouse's coordinates from the changing image patterns as it moves → the computer moves the on-screen cursor to match.
Red LED light reflects off the surface → CMOS sensor → DSP calculates position → cursor moves.
Benefits vs. mechanical mouse
  • No moving parts — more reliable
  • Dirt can't get trapped in mechanical components
  • No need for a special surface
Wired vs. Bluetooth optical mouse
  • Wired: no signal loss (constant wire pathway), cheaper (no batteries), fewer environmental issues (no battery disposal)
  • Bluetooth: more versatile (no cable), but most modern optical mice use it despite these trade-offs
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — the optical mouse.

Short answer 1
What role does the CMOS play in an optical mouse?
Reveal answer

The CMOS picks up the red light reflected off the surface and generates electric pulses representing that reflected light.

Short answer 2
How many images per second does an optical mouse's camera take?
Reveal answer

Up to 1,500 images per second.

Fill in the blank 3
The ______ works out the mouse's coordinates based on the changing image patterns as it moves across the surface.
Reveal answer

The DSP (Digital Signal Processor).

Spot the error 4
A student claims: "A wired optical mouse has no real advantages over a Bluetooth one, since Bluetooth is simply more modern and better." What real advantages does this ignore?
Reveal answer

The lesson lists real advantages of a WIRED mouse: no signal loss (constant wire connection), cheaper to operate (no batteries to buy/charge), and fewer environmental issues (no battery disposal) — Bluetooth is more versatile, but genuinely trades away these specific benefits.

Applied / scenario 5
A user wants a mouse that can work reliably on almost any desk surface, including uneven or textured ones, without needing a special mouse mat. Which type of mouse — mechanical or optical — should they choose, and why, based on this lesson's content?
Reveal answer

Optical. The lesson states an optical mouse can work on virtually ANY surface (unlike an older mechanical mouse), and that no special surface is needed — this directly matches the user's requirement.

Short answer 6
Name one real advantage an optical mouse has over a mechanical mouse, other than working on any surface.
Reveal answer

Any of: no moving parts (more reliable), dirt cannot get trapped in mechanical components.
