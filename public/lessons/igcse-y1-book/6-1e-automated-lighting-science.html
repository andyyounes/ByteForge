6.1e Automated Systems: Lighting & Science
A garden that lights itself up at dusk, and a lab that mixes chemicals with zero human hands — completing section 6.1.
Theory

Example 6 — automated house lighting

  • Garden lights turn on automatically when someone enters or it gets dark. A microprocessor-controlled water fountain runs a lighting show only after dark.
  • As it gets dark, the light sensor's value changes, and the microprocessor signals the LED array around the garden via an interface.
  • An INFRARED sensor also acts as a security device day and night whenever the house is unoccupied.
  • The fountain lighting show follows a PRE-PROGRAMMED colour display; the microprocessor also signals actuators to turn the fountain's water pumps on/off.
Lighting — advantages
  • Automatic light control
  • Reduced energy consumption (lights only on when needed)
  • Wireless connections are safer (no trailing wires)
  • Longer bulb life (dimming/switching off when unused)
  • New light displays can be programmed for occasions
Lighting — disadvantages
  • Expensive initial set-up
  • Wireless connections (chosen for safety) can be less reliable than wired
  • More maintenance needed for consistent performance

Example 7 — automated pharmaceutical lab experiment

  • A burette ('A') adds one chemical (tap operated by a small ACTUATOR) into a reaction vessel ('B'). The reaction turns bright orange when complete.
  • LEVEL SENSORS measure how much liquid is added; a COLORIMETER checks the resulting colour of the reaction. Both send data to the microprocessor.
  • The microprocessor controls the tap's opening/closing via the actuator — the WHOLE experiment runs with NO human interaction.
  • Great benefit to pharmaceutical companies developing drugs/vaccines: multiple experiments can run SIMULTANEOUSLY, with just one person monitoring the whole system.
Lab experiment — advantages
  • More consistent/repeatable results
  • Less dangerous (especially with hazardous chemicals)
  • Faster — multiple experiments run simultaneously
  • Automatic analysis of results
  • Fewer highly-trained staff needed per experiment
  • Results can be monitored anywhere in the world, in real time
Lab experiment — disadvantages
  • Less flexible than human technicians
  • Security risks when sharing data globally
  • Expensive equipment to buy/set up

AI in automated systems — a quick preview

  • Advantages: accessing/storing VAST amounts of facts; learning from huge datasets that would overwhelm humans (or take them months/years); spotting patterns humans might MISS.
  • Disadvantages: a shift in who's really "in control" (human vs. AI); AI's output depends entirely on the DATA that trained it.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — automated lighting and science (lab) systems.

Short answer 1
In the automated garden lighting example, what triggers the microprocessor to switch the LED array on?
Reveal answer

The light sensor's value changing as it gets dark — this signals the microprocessor to activate the LED array via the interface.

Short answer 2
What real instrument checks the colour of the reacting chemicals in the pharmaceutical lab example, and what does it send its readings to?
Reveal answer

A colorimeter, sending its readings to the microprocessor.

Fill in the blank 3
In the lab experiment, the microprocessor controls the tap on burette 'A' by sending signals to an ______, which operates the tap.
Reveal answer

Actuator.

Spot the error 4
A student claims: "Automated lab experiments are always MORE flexible than using human technicians, since computers can react instantly." What's wrong with this?
Reveal answer

The lesson explicitly lists reduced FLEXIBILITY (compared to human technicians) as a real disadvantage of the automated lab system — reacting quickly to programmed conditions is not the same as having a human's adaptability to genuinely novel or unexpected situations.

Applied / scenario 5
A pharmaceutical company wants to run 10 identical drug-development experiments simultaneously, with minimal staff. Using this lesson's content, explain why the automated laboratory system described here is well-suited to this.
Reveal answer

The lesson explicitly states this type of automated system allows several experiments to be carried out at the same time with only ONE person needed to monitor the whole system — a direct match for running 10 simultaneous experiments with minimal staff, while also giving more consistent/repeatable results across all 10 runs.

Short answer 6
Name one genuine disadvantage of using AI within these automated systems, according to this lesson.
Reveal answer

Either: a shift in who's really in control (human vs. AI), or that AI's usefulness is entirely dependent on the quality/nature of the data it was trained on.
