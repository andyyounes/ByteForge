3.2i Output Devices: Inkjet Printers
From print head to paper — thermal bubble and piezoelectric ink ejection, and the full printing sequence.
Key Terms
Print head
Contains nozzles that spray tiny ink droplets onto the paper to form characters/images.
Thermal bubble
An inkjet technology using heat to vaporise ink into a bubble, ejecting a droplet as it expands.
Piezoelectric
An inkjet technology using an electrically-charged vibrating crystal to eject ink droplets.
Printer buffer
Temporary memory where print data is stored before/during printing.
Theory

Inkjet printer structure

  • Print head: nozzles spraying ink droplets to form characters.
  • Ink cartridges: either separate cyan/magenta/yellow + black cartridges, or one combined cartridge (some systems use 6 colours).
  • Stepper motor and belt: moves the print head assembly side to side across the page.
  • Paper feed: automatically feeds pages as needed.

Two ink-ejection technologies

  • Thermal bubble: tiny resistors heat locally, vaporising ink into a bubble. As the bubble expands, ink is ejected onto the paper. When it collapses, a small vacuum draws in fresh ink — repeating for the whole print cycle.
  • Piezoelectric: a crystal behind each nozzle's ink reservoir is given a tiny electric charge, making it VIBRATE. This vibration forces ink out onto the paper, while more ink is simultaneously drawn in.
The inkjet printing sequence (both technologies follow these same steps)
  1. Document data sent to the printer driver
  2. Driver converts data into a format the printer understands
  3. Driver checks the printer is available (not busy/offline/out of ink)
  4. Data sent to printer, stored in the printer buffer
  5. Paper fed in; sensor checks paper is available (error if out/jammed)
  6. Print head moves side to side, spraying exact ink amounts for the desired colour
  7. After each pass, paper advances slightly for the next line
  8. Repeat steps 5-7 until the buffer is empty
  9. Printer sends an interrupt to the CPU requesting more data — repeats until the whole document is printed

When to use an inkjet printer

  • Best suited to: one-off photos, or small runs of good-quality colour printing, where small ink cartridges/paper trays aren't a real limitation.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — inkjet printers.

Short answer 1
In thermal bubble technology, what actually causes the ink to be ejected from the nozzle?
Reveal answer

Tiny resistors heat locally, vaporising ink into a bubble; as the bubble EXPANDS, some ink is forced/ejected out of the print head onto the paper.

Short answer 2
What role does the printer buffer play in the printing process?
Reveal answer

It's temporary memory where the print data is stored after being sent to the printer, so it's ready to be printed even before/while the physical printing happens.

Fill in the blank pseudocode 3
Once the printer buffer is empty, the printer sends an ______ to the CPU, requesting ______ data.
Reveal answer

Blank 1: interrupt. Blank 2: more.

Spot the error 4
A student claims: "In piezoelectric inkjet technology, heat is used to vaporise the ink into a bubble, just like thermal bubble technology." What's wrong with this?
Reveal answer

Piezoelectric technology does NOT use heat at all — it uses an electric charge to make a CRYSTAL vibrate, and that vibration is what forces ink out. Heat-based bubble formation is specifically the THERMAL BUBBLE method, a genuinely different mechanism.

Applied / scenario 5
A user sends a document to print, but the printer's paper tray is actually empty. Based on this lesson's step-by-step process, at which stage does the system detect this, and what happens?
Reveal answer

At the paper-feed stage (step 5), a sensor detects whether paper is available in the feed tray. Since it's empty, an ERROR MESSAGE is sent back to the computer — the printing process cannot continue for that page until paper is added.

Short answer 6
For what kind of printing task is an inkjet printer particularly well suited, according to this lesson?
Reveal answer

One-off photos, or a small number of good-quality colour pages — where the smaller ink cartridges/paper trays aren't a practical limitation.
