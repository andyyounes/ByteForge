6.2b Robotics: Autonomous Transport
Cars, trains, and planes that drive, ride, and fly themselves — and why humans still hesitate.
Key Terms
LiDAR
Light Detection and Ranging — uses lasers to build a 3D image of surroundings, used in autonomous trains.
Phantom traffic jam
Stop-and-go congestion caused by human driving error, which autonomous vehicles can smooth out.
Theory

Autonomous cars — how they work

  • Use sensors (radar, ultrasonics) and cameras as the car's "eyes and ears", building a 3D image of surroundings — vital in poor visibility (rain, fog, night).
  • Microprocessors process this data and send signals to actuators for: changing gear, applying brakes, turning the wheel.
  • Example: approaching a red light — the car recognises the sign, checks its database, applies brakes and parks; constantly monitors until green, then releases brakes and moves off (checking for hazards like a broken-down car ahead or a crossing pedestrian).
Autonomous cars — advantages
  • Safer — human error removed
  • Better for the environment (more efficient operation)
  • Reduced traffic congestion (smooths out "phantom traffic jams")
  • +100% lane capacity, +20% average speed (better braking/spacing)
  • Reduced travel/commute times
  • Stress-free self-parking
Autonomous cars — disadvantages
  • Very expensive to set up
  • Fear of hacking into the control system
  • Security/safety risk from software glitches
  • Cameras/sensors can fail (dirt, heavy snow deflecting radar)
  • Driver/passenger reluctance to trust the technology
  • Could cause taxi driver unemployment

Autonomous trains

  • Use LiDAR (laser-based 3D imaging), other sensors (e.g. door proximity sensors), cameras (including infrared), and GPS for speed/direction calculations.
  • Already widely accepted in many major cities — partly because trains follow a FIXED track and don't overtake, unlike cars.
Autonomous trains — advantages
  • Improved punctuality
  • Reduced running costs (fewer staff)
  • Improved safety (human error removed)
  • Lower energy consumption (better speed control)
  • More frequent trains possible; easier to reschedule
Autonomous trains — disadvantages
  • Fear of hacking
  • Doesn't yet work well on very busy services
  • High initial capital/operational costs
  • Passenger behaviour issues (jamming doors, platform edge risk)
  • Passenger reluctance
  • Needs CCTV for station monitoring without drivers

Autonomous (pilotless) airplanes

  • Existing autopilots already handle most of a flight; pilots mainly take over for take-off/landing.
  • A fully pilotless plane would add: turbulence detection sensors, increased self-testing of circuits/systems, cabin depressurisation detection, GPS navigation/speed, and actuators controlling throttle, wing flaps, and rudder.
Pilotless planes — advantages
  • Improved passenger comfort
  • Reduced running costs (fewer staff)
  • Improved safety (most crashes are pilot-error related)
  • Better aerodynamics (no cockpit needed)
Pilotless planes — disadvantages
  • Security risk with no pilot on board (e.g. hijack scenarios)
  • Harder to handle unexpected emergencies
  • Hacking risk to flight controls
  • Passenger reluctance
  • Software glitches can be catastrophic
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — autonomous cars, trains, and airplanes.

Short answer 1
What technology do autonomous trains use to build a 3D image of their surroundings using lasers?
Reveal answer

LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging).

Short answer 2
Why have autonomous trains been more widely accepted by the public than autonomous cars?
Reveal answer

Trains follow a fixed, specific track and don't overtake other trains — a genuinely simpler, more constrained environment than road traffic, which likely makes people more comfortable trusting the automation.

Fill in the blank 3
A "phantom traffic jam" is caused by ______ error, which autonomous vehicles can help smooth out by better regulating speed and following distance.
Reveal answer

Human error (stop-and-go driving behaviour).

Spot the error 4
A student claims: "Pilotless planes remove all risk of a crash, since there's no human pilot to make an error." What's wrong with this?
Reveal answer

The lesson lists genuine remaining risks even without a human pilot: hacking into flight controls, software glitches (which have had "devastating results" in real modern planes), and difficulty handling unexpected emergencies — removing pilot error doesn't eliminate crash risk entirely.

Applied / scenario 5
A city is considering introducing autonomous trains on a very busy commuter line running trains every 90 seconds at peak times. Using this lesson's content, identify one real limitation they should consider.
Reveal answer

The lesson explicitly notes that autonomous train systems don't currently work well with VERY BUSY services — this is a real limitation the city should weigh against the benefits of automation for this particular high-frequency line.

Short answer 6
Name two actuator-controlled functions in an autonomous car when approaching a red traffic light.
Reveal answer

Applying the brakes, and putting the gear into "park" (later, releasing brakes and operating the throttle when the light turns green).
