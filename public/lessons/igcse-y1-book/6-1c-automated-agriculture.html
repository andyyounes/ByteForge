6.1c Automated Systems: Agriculture
Watering a 10km² field with no one there — wireless irrigation control from Brazil.
Theory

Example 5 — automated Brazilian irrigation system

  • Fully automatic crop irrigation, relying heavily on WIRELESS transmission — allowing use across huge, remote fields (some over 10 km²).
  • An automatic WEATHER STATION sends data to the controller every 10 minutes, especially important for very wet/dry conditions.
  • Ultrasonic WATER LEVEL SENSORS in the irrigation channels measure water amounts, sending data via wireless transmitters to a wireless receiver, then on to the CONTROLLER.
  • The controller combines this with weather data to decide whether to START/STOP water pumps — done via signals to ACTUATORS operating the pumps.
  • A SUPERVISOR still monitors remotely via a schematic on-screen, overseeing MULTIPLE irrigation systems from one central point, and can OVERRIDE the controller if needed.
Weather station + water-level sensors feed the controller; the controller signals pumps; the supervisor oversees from a remote location.
Advantages
  • Reduced labour costs — one supervisor covers vast areas
  • Better, more efficient irrigation control
  • Better control of precious resources (water)
  • Faster response than manual checking of long channels
  • Safer (fields can reach 40°C+)
  • Can maintain different crop-specific conditions simultaneously (e.g. rice needs flooding, oranges need dry conditions)
Disadvantages
  • Expensive equipment/set-up
  • High maintenance costs, needs specialist technicians (a problem in remote areas)
  • Channels must be well-maintained — a blocked/collapsed channel wouldn't be detected, causing over- or under-watering
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — automated agriculture (irrigation).

Short answer 1
Why is wireless transmission such an important part of this irrigation system?
Reveal answer

It allows the system to work across very remote, vast areas (some fields over 10 km²) where running physical cables between sensors and the controller would be impractical.

Short answer 2
How does the controller decide whether to start or stop the water pumps?
Reveal answer

It combines data from the water level sensors (irrigation channel levels) with data from the automatic weather station (updated every 10 minutes) to decide, then sends signals to actuators that operate the pumps.

Fill in the blank 3
A single supervisor can oversee ______ irrigation systems from one central point, and can ______ the controller if needed.
Reveal answer

Blank 1: several/multiple. Blank 2: override.

Spot the error 4
A student claims: "The system will always correctly detect if a field is being under-watered, since it's fully automated." What real gap in the lesson's content contradicts this?
Reveal answer

The lesson explicitly notes that a blocked or collapsed irrigation CHANNEL would NOT be picked up by the automated system, which could result in some areas being over-watered and others starved of water — the system monitors sensor readings, not the physical integrity of the channels themselves.

Applied / scenario 5
A farm grows both rice (which needs flooded fields) and orange trees (which need dry conditions) on the same irrigation network. Using this lesson's content, explain how the automated system can manage both crops' very different needs at the same time.
Reveal answer

The controllers can be PROGRAMMED so that different growing conditions are maintained simultaneously — this is explicitly given as an advantage, since different crops (like flood-loving rice vs. dry-loving oranges) can each have their own irrigation requirements managed independently within the same overall automated system.

Short answer 6
How often does the automatic weather station send data to the irrigation controller?
Reveal answer

Every 10 minutes.
