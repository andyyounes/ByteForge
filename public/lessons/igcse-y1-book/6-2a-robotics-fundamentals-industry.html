6.2a Robotics: Fundamentals & Industrial Use
What actually makes something a "robot" — and how factories use them for welding, spraying, and 24/7 precision work.
Key Terms
Robotics
A branch of computer science bringing together the design, construction, and operation of robots.
Controller
A robot's programmable "brain", deciding actions based on sensor/camera data.
End-effector
An attachment on a robotic arm for a specific task (welding, spraying, cutting, lifting).
Independent (autonomous) robot
No direct human control — can fully replace human activity.
Dependent robot
Needs a human interface — supplements rather than replaces human activity.
Theory

The three characteristics of a true robot

  • Sense its surroundings: via sensors (light, pressure, temperature, acoustic, etc.) — recognising size, shape, weight, temperature of objects, all sent to a microprocessor.
  • Have a degree of movement: wheels, cogs, pistons, gears for turning/twisting/moving/gripping/lifting — built from motors, hydraulics, actuators, circuit boards; may use END-EFFECTORS for specific tasks.
  • Be programmable: a controller ("brain") decides actions based on sensor/camera data, and can be programmed for different tasks.
  • Important distinction: robotics and AI are largely SEPARATE fields — many robots have NO AI, since they simply repeat programmed tasks rather than adapting like a human would.
  • SOFTWARE robots (search engine bots, chat bots) are NOT true robots under this definition — they lack the physical sensing/movement characteristics.

Independent vs. dependent robots

  • Independent (autonomous): no direct human control at all — can fully replace human activity (e.g. an autonomous vehicle).
  • Dependent: a human interfaces directly (computer/control panel) — SUPPLEMENTS rather than replaces human activity (e.g. humans and robots working together on a car assembly line).

Robots in industry

  • Used for: paint spraying, welding, microchip manufacturing, electrical goods manufacturing, automatic warehouses.
  • Programmed either directly with an instruction SEQUENCE, or by a human manually GUIDING the robot arm (or wearing sensors themselves) — each movement recorded and stored, then replayed identically every time.
  • Sensors prevent robots doing "stupid things" — e.g. stopping spraying if no car is present, or if paint runs out.
  • Robots excel at REPETITIVE tasks; specialist "thinking" tasks (e.g. handmade scientific glassware) are often still better done by humans.
Industrial robots — advantages
  • Work in hazardous conditions safely
  • Run 24/7 without stopping
  • Cheaper long-term (fewer salaries)
  • More productive than humans
  • More CONSISTENT (not necessarily more accurate)
  • Better suited to boring, repetitive tasks (fewer mistakes)
  • Lower heating/lighting costs (robots don't need warmth/good light)
Industrial robots — disadvantages
  • Struggle with "non-standard" tasks (e.g. a cracked windscreen)
  • Can lead to higher unemployment in manual labour
  • Risk of human DESKILLING as robots take over tasks
  • Factories can relocate to cheaper countries (further unemployment)
  • Expensive to buy and set up initially
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — robotics fundamentals and industrial use.

Short answer 1
Name the three characteristics something must have to be correctly called a robot.
Reveal answer

Ability to sense its surroundings; have a degree of movement; be programmable (via a controller).

Short answer 2
What is the key difference between an independent robot and a dependent robot?
Reveal answer

An independent robot has NO direct human control and can fully REPLACE human activity. A dependent robot needs a human interfacing directly with it, and SUPPLEMENTS (rather than replaces) human activity.

Fill in the blank 3
A ______ is an attachment on a robotic arm allowing it to perform specific tasks like welding or spraying.
Reveal answer

End-effector.

Spot the error 4
A student claims: "A website chatbot is a true robot, since it interacts with users like a real robot would." What's wrong with this?
Reveal answer

Chatbots are SOFTWARE robots, not physical ones — they lack the ability to physically sense surroundings or move, which are two of the three defining characteristics of a true robot. According to this lesson's definition, software robots like chatbots don't count as true robots.

Applied / scenario 5
A car factory uses robots for welding car bodies, but keeps human workers for fitting delicate custom trim that varies slightly on every vehicle. Using this lesson's content, explain why this division of labour makes sense.
Reveal answer

Robots excel at REPETITIVE tasks like welding, which is identical every time — a perfect fit for consistent, tireless robot work. But the lesson notes robots can find "non-standard" tasks difficult, and specialist tasks requiring adaptable "thinking" are often better handled by humans — exactly matching delicate, variable trim-fitting work.

Short answer 6
Give one real reason industrial robots can lead to unemployment, according to this lesson.
Reveal answer

Either: robots directly replacing manual labour jobs, or factories relocating to countries with lower operation costs since labour location matters less with robots — both cause unemployment.
