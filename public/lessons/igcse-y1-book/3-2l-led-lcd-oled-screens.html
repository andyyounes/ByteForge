3.2l LED, LCD & OLED Screens
Why a "LED TV" is usually not what it sounds like, and how OLED removed the need for backlighting entirely.
Key Terms
LED screen
Made of tiny light-emitting diodes (red/green/blue), each individually producing light — used for large outdoor displays.
LCD screen
Made of tiny liquid crystals affected by electric fields; produces NO light itself — needs backlighting.
CCFL
Cold Cathode Fluorescent Lamp — the older backlighting method for LCD screens, before LEDs.
OLED
Organic LED — a flexible, self-lighting screen technology needing no backlighting at all.
Theory

LED screens vs. "LED-backlit" LCD screens (a common mix-up)

  • A true LED screen is made of tiny LEDs (red/green/blue), each independently varied in brightness to produce colour — used for large outdoor displays due to their brilliant colours.
  • An LCD screen is made of tiny liquid crystals affected by electric fields — but LCD crystals produce NO light of their own, so they need BACKLIGHTING.
  • Important warning: many TVs advertised as "LED" are actually LCD screens that are simply BACKLIT using LEDs — this is genuinely different from a true LED screen, and easy to mix up.
  • Before LEDs, LCD backlighting used CCFL (Cold Cathode Fluorescent Lamp) — two fluorescent tubes behind the screen.
LED backlighting advantages over CCFL

LEDs reach full brightness almost instantly (no "warm up"); give a whiter light (sharper image, more vivid colour — CCFL had a yellowish tint); produce brighter light (better colour definition); allow much THINNER monitors; last indefinitely (more reliable); and consume less power (less heat, less energy).

OLED (Organic LED)

  • OLEDs use ORGANIC materials (carbon compounds) as flexible semiconductors. An organic film is sandwiched between two charged electrodes (a metallic cathode and a glass anode) — applying an electric field makes them EMIT LIGHT DIRECTLY.
  • Since OLEDs generate their OWN light, no backlighting is needed at all — this means OLED is genuinely self-contained, unlike LCD.
  • Because OLED layers can be plastic rather than glass, screens can be extremely thin and even BENT into curved or flexible shapes.
OLED advantages over LED and LCD

Thinner, lighter, more flexible plastic layers (vs. crystal structures in LED/LCD); brighter light than LED; no backlighting needed (unlike LCD) — meaning much LESS power use, crucial for battery devices like phones; can be made into large thin sheets (e.g. advertising boards); and a very wide field of view (~170°), ideal for TVs and advertising screens.

Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — LED, LCD, and OLED screens.

Short answer 1
Explain the common mix-up between "LED TVs" and LCD screens.
Reveal answer

Many TVs advertised as "LED" are actually LCD screens that are simply BACKLIT using LEDs, not true LED screens where every pixel is its own LED. The underlying display technology is still LCD — the LEDs are only providing the light source behind it.

Short answer 2
Why does an LCD screen need backlighting at all, unlike an OLED screen?
Reveal answer

LCD's liquid crystals do not produce any light of their own — they only control light passing through them, so a separate light source (backlighting) is required. OLEDs, by contrast, generate their own light directly, so they need no backlighting at all.

Fill in the blank 3
Before LED backlighting, LCD screens used ______ (a fluorescent-tube technology) as their light source.
Reveal answer

CCFL (Cold Cathode Fluorescent Lamp).

Spot the error 4
A student claims: "OLED screens still need LED backlighting, just like modern LCD TVs." What's wrong with this?
Reveal answer

OLEDs generate their OWN light directly through the organic emissive layer — they require NO backlighting whatsoever. This is a key distinguishing feature of OLED technology, genuinely different from LCD, which always needs some external light source (LED or CCFL).

Applied / scenario 5
A mobile phone manufacturer wants to design a phone with a screen that can curve around the wrist like a watch strap, while also maximising battery life. Which screen technology should they choose, and why, using this lesson's content?
Reveal answer

OLED. The lesson explicitly describes OLED's flexibility (plastic layers can bend into curved/wrap-around shapes, e.g. a watch-strap-style phone) and its much lower power consumption compared to LCD (since no backlighting is needed) — both requirements are directly met by OLED.

Short answer 6
Name one real advantage of LED backlighting over the older CCFL method.
Reveal answer

Any of: near-instant full brightness (no warm-up); whiter, more vivid light; brighter light/better colour definition; thinner screens possible; longer-lasting/more reliable; lower power consumption.
