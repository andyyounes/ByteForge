5.3b DDoS Attacks & Hacking
Flooding a server until it can't answer anyone — and the difference between illegal hacking and ethical hacking.
Key Terms
DoS attack
Denial of Service — preventing users from accessing part of a network (e.g. a server).
DDoS attack
Distributed DoS — spam traffic originates from MANY different computers, making it hard to block.
Hacking
Gaining illegal access to a computer system without permission.
Ethical hacking
Companies paying authorised hackers to legitimately test their own system's security.
Theory

Denial of Service (DoS) attacks

  • Attempts to prevent users accessing part of a network (notably an internet server) — usually TEMPORARY but potentially very damaging.
  • Can target individuals too, blocking access to: emails, websites/web pages, online services (e.g. banking).
  • Method: flood the network with USELESS spam traffic. A server can only handle a FINITE number of requests — if overloaded with thousands of fake requests, it can't service legitimate ones.

Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS)

  • The spam traffic originates from MANY different computers — making the attack much harder to BLOCK (no single source to stop).
  • Can also target an individual's email: attacker sends thousands of spam messages, exceeding the user's ISP data quota, clogging their inbox and blocking legitimate emails.
DDoS traffic floods a server from many different sources at once — much harder to block than a single-source attack.

Guarding against DDoS

  • Up-to-date MALWARE checker.
  • FIREWALL to restrict traffic to/from the server or computer.
  • EMAIL FILTERS to filter out unwanted traffic (e.g. spam).
  • Warning signs of a DDoS victim: slow network performance, inability to access certain sites, large volumes of spam email.

Hacking

  • Gaining ILLEGAL access to a computer system without permission — can lead to identity theft, personal info theft; data can be deleted, passed on, changed, or corrupted.
  • Encryption does NOT stop hacking — it makes data meaningless to the hacker, but doesn't stop them deleting/corrupting/passing it on.
  • Prevented via: firewalls, user names, frequently-changed strong passwords, anti-hacking software, intrusion-detection software.
  • Malicious hacking: always ILLEGAL, done without permission.
  • Ethical hacking: companies AUTHORISE paid hackers to test their own security measures/robustness — a legitimate, professional practice.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — DDoS attacks and hacking.

Short answer 1
Why is a DDoS attack harder to block than a regular DoS attack?
Reveal answer

In a DDoS, the spam traffic comes from MANY different computers rather than one single source — there's no single origin to block, making the attack much harder to stop.

Short answer 2
What is the key difference between malicious hacking and ethical hacking?
Reveal answer

Malicious hacking is always ILLEGAL and done WITHOUT the owner's permission. Ethical hacking is AUTHORISED by the company itself, who pays hackers to legitimately test their own security systems.

Fill in the blank 3
Encryption does NOT stop ______; it only makes the data ______ to the hacker without the decryption key.
Reveal answer

Blank 1: hacking. Blank 2: meaningless.

Spot the error 4
A student claims: "Encrypting all your data completely stops hackers from deleting or corrupting it." What's wrong with this?
Reveal answer

Encryption only makes DATA CONTENT meaningless to a hacker without the decryption key — it does NOT stop them from deleting, corrupting, or passing on the data itself. Encryption protects confidentiality of content, not the data's existence/integrity from deletion or tampering.

Applied / scenario 5
A user notices their internet is unusually slow, certain websites won't load at all, and their email inbox is flooded with spam. Using this lesson's content, identify what's likely happening and one real defence they could use.
Reveal answer

These are the exact warning signs of a DDoS attack listed in this lesson: slow network performance, inability to access certain websites, and large amounts of spam email. Real defences: an up-to-date malware checker, a firewall to restrict traffic, and email filters to block spam.

Short answer 6
Why do universities and companies now run courses in ethical hacking?
Reveal answer

So companies can authorise paid hackers to legitimately test how robust their own computer systems are against hacking attacks — a professional, legal way of finding security weaknesses before malicious hackers do.
