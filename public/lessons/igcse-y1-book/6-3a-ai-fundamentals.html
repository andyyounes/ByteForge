6.3a Artificial Intelligence: Fundamentals & Examples
Narrow, general, and strong AI — and how Alexa, chatbots, and facial recognition all count as real-world AI.
Key Terms
Artificial Intelligence (AI)
A branch of computer science simulating intelligent human behaviour (the brain's cognitive functions) in a computer.
Narrow AI
A machine outperforming a human at ONE specific task.
General AI
A machine performing SIMILARLY (not superior) to a human at a specific task.
Strong AI
A machine outperforming humans across MANY tasks.
Deductive reasoning
Building correct facts into rules, then applying those rules to new, related problems.
Theory

What AI is

  • A branch of computer science simulating intelligent human behaviour — the "cognitive functions" of the brain (acquiring knowledge/understanding through thought, experience, the five senses).
  • AI is measured against human benchmarks: reasoning, speech, sight.
  • At its core, AI is a collection of RULES and DATA, plus the ability to reason, learn, and adapt to external stimuli.

Three categories of AI

  • Narrow AI: superior to a human at ONE specific task.
  • General AI: similar (not superior) to a human at a specific task.
  • Strong AI: superior to a human across MANY tasks.

Reasoning and learning

  • Deductive reasoning: correct facts are built into a set of RULES, then applied to other problems — e.g. an AI that learns to make a perfect cup of tea can apply the same learned rules/methodology to making coffee or hot chocolate, adjusting where needed.
  • Through repeated steps, the AI system learns and applies what it learns to NEW situations more effectively over time.
  • AI can spot patterns very quickly (sometimes patterns humans CAN'T see), then make predictions by adapting to new data.
Real-world AI examples

News generation: based on live news feeds.

Smart home devices (Alexa, Google Now, Siri, Cortana): recognise verbal commands, learn from the environment/data received, becoming more sophisticated over time — via repetitive learning. Voice is converted to binary for processing, and a verbal response is generated.

Chatbots: interact through instant messaging, artificially replicating human interaction patterns, responding to typed/voice messages using currently-known information.

Autonomous cars: covered in section 6.2.

Facial expression recognition: algorithms identify key facial landmarks (eyebrow/mouth corners, etc.); combining these landmarks maps emotions like anger, fear, joy, surprise.

Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — AI fundamentals and real-world examples.

Short answer 1
What is the difference between narrow AI and strong AI?
Reveal answer

Narrow AI outperforms a human at only ONE specific task. Strong AI outperforms humans across MANY tasks.

Short answer 2
Explain "deductive reasoning" using the tea/coffee example from this lesson.
Reveal answer

Deductive reasoning builds correct facts into a set of rules — an AI that learns to make the perfect cup of tea from a set of facts can then APPLY those same learned rules (adjusted where necessary) to making coffee or hot chocolate, without starting from scratch each time.

Fill in the blank 3
A machine that performs SIMILARLY (not superior) to a human at a specific task is classed as ______ AI.
Reveal answer

General AI.

Spot the error 4
A student claims: "Facial expression recognition works by having AI directly 'read minds' to detect emotion." What real mechanism does this misunderstand?
Reveal answer

Facial expression recognition works by algorithms identifying specific FACIAL LANDMARKS (eyebrow corners, mouth corners, etc.) and mapping COMBINATIONS of these landmarks to known emotions — it's analysing observable physical facial features, not reading minds.

Applied / scenario 5
A chess-playing AI consistently beats the best human grandmasters, but performs no better than an average human at general conversation. Using this lesson's content, classify this AI, and justify your answer.
Reveal answer

This is narrow AI — it has superior performance to humans at ONE specific task (chess), but does not outperform humans generally (its conversation ability is only average), which rules out strong AI or general AI classification for its OVERALL behaviour.

Short answer 6
Name three human benchmarks AI capability is measured against, according to this lesson.
Reveal answer

Reasoning, speech, and sight.
