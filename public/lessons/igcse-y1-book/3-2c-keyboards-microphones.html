3.2c Input Devices: Keyboards & Microphones
Turning a key press into ASCII, and a sound wave into an electric current — two of the most common input methods.
Key Terms
RSI
Repetitive strain injury — hand/wrist injury from frequent keyboard use.
Ergonomic keyboard
A keyboard with a different key layout, designed to reduce strain and better support wrists/hands.
Diaphragm
The vibrating part of a microphone that picks up sound-wave air vibrations.
ADC (in microphones)
Analogue-to-Digital Converter — turns the microphone's analogue electric current into digital values a computer can store.
Theory

Keyboards

  • Keyboards are by far the most common data-entry method — used on computers, tablets, phones, and more. Connection is via USB, wireless, or (on tablets/phones) a virtual touchscreen keyboard.
  • Each character has an ASCII value; each key press is converted into a digital signal the computer interprets.
  • Keyboards are relatively SLOW and error-prone, but remain the easiest way to enter text.
  • Frequent use can cause repetitive strain injury (RSI) in hands/wrists. Ergonomic keyboards — with a different key layout and better wrist/hand support — help reduce this risk.
  • How a key press is recognised: a membrane/circuit board sits at the base of the keys. Pressing a key COMPLETES A CIRCUIT. The CPU determines which key was pressed, looks it up in an index file, and matches it to its ASCII value.

Microphones

  • Microphones are built-in or external (USB/Bluetooth), converting sound waves into an electric current, which is then digitised for storage/processing.
  • How it works: sound creates air vibrations → these vibrate the microphone's diaphragm → a copper coil (wrapped around a cone connected to the diaphragm) moves back and forth → this motion cuts through the magnetic field of a permanent magnet, INDUCING an electric current.
  • This electric current is ANALOGUE in nature — it's either amplified or sent to a recording device.
  • To store the sound digitally, a sound card (or ADC) converts the analogue current into digital values.
Sound waves vibrate the diaphragm/coil, inducing an analogue current, which an ADC converts to digital data.
Example — word "HUT" as sound wave becomes digital data

The sound wave for "HUT", captured by the microphone, is converted via ADC into a sequence of binary values (e.g. 1000 0001, 0001 1110, ...) which can then be stored or manipulated by software.

Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — keyboards and microphones.

Short answer 1
How does the CPU determine which key was pressed on a keyboard?
Reveal answer

Pressing a key completes a circuit on the membrane/circuit board beneath the keys. The CPU detects this and looks the key up in an index file to determine which character (and its ASCII value) the key press represents.

Short answer 2
What real health problem can frequent keyboard use cause, and what type of keyboard is designed to reduce this risk?
Reveal answer

Repetitive strain injury (RSI) in the hands/wrists. An ergonomic keyboard is designed to reduce this risk.

Fill in the blank 3
As the diaphragm vibrates, the attached coil moves through the ______ field of a permanent magnet, ______ an electric current.
Reveal answer

Blank 1: magnetic. Blank 2: inducing.

Spot the error 4
A student claims: "The electric current straight out of a microphone is already in digital form, ready to be stored on a computer." What's wrong with this?
Reveal answer

The current coming directly out of a microphone is ANALOGUE, not digital. It must be passed through an ADC (or a sound card) to be converted into digital values before a computer can store or process it.

Applied / scenario 5
An office worker who types for many hours a day starts experiencing wrist pain. Using this lesson's content, name the condition they likely have, and one hardware solution that could help.
Reveal answer

They likely have repetitive strain injury (RSI), caused by frequent keyboard use. An ergonomic keyboard — with its different key layout and better wrist/hand support — is the hardware solution this lesson identifies as helping to reduce this risk.

Short answer 6
List, in order, the physical chain of events inside a microphone from sound arriving to a current being produced.
Reveal answer

Sound vibrates the air → air vibrations make the diaphragm vibrate → the diaphragm's attached cone/coil moves back and forth → this motion cuts through the permanent magnet's magnetic field → an electric current is induced.
