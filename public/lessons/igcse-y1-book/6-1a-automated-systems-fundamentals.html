6.1a Automated Systems: Fundamentals & Industrial Applications
Sensors + microprocessor + actuators = a system that runs itself — a nuclear power station and a paracetamol factory, both explained the same way.
Key Terms
Automated system
Software + hardware (sensors, microprocessors, actuators) designed to work WITHOUT human intervention — though often still monitored.
DCS
Distributed Control System — a powerful computer monitoring/controlling an entire industrial process with no human interaction needed.
Theory

The basic automated-system pattern

  • Sensors (input devices) take readings from their surroundings, sending data to a microprocessor. Analogue data is first converted via ADC.
  • The microprocessor processes this data and decides on the necessary action based on its programming.
  • The result is OUTPUT — usually signals sent to actuators to control motors, wheels, solenoids, etc.
  • Every example in this unit follows this same sensor → microprocessor → actuator pattern, just applied to a different real process.
Real example 1 — a nuclear power station

Sensors used: temperature, pressure, flow level, gas (escape), and radiation level.

All sensor data feeds into the DCS (Distributed Control System) — analogue data converted via ADC first. The DCS checks this against a large database of operational parameters, and if action is needed, sends signals to actuators controlling valves, water pumps, gas pumps, or even an automatic shutdown.

A human SUPERVISOR sits in a remote control room, watching a schematic on a large screen — the process is fully automatic, but the supervisor CAN override the DCS and shut it down manually.

Real example 2 — manufacturing paracetamol

Two monitored processes: Process 1 (manufacturing the paracetamol) and Process 2 (making solid tablets). Sensors on both feed data to a central computer, which checks its database and sends signals to actuators (pumps, valves, heaters, stirrers, pistons) to keep both processes within correct parameters — again, fully automated but overridable by a remote operator.

Advantages (both examples)
  • Much faster than a human operator
  • Much safer — timely interventions, keeps humans out of danger
  • Process runs closer to optimum conditions
  • Cheaper long-term (replaces round-the-clock human monitoring)
  • More efficient material use, higher productivity, more consistent results (paracetamol example)
Disadvantages (both examples)
  • Expensive to set up, needs extensive testing
  • Untested condition combinations could still arise (safety risk) — hence the human supervisor
  • Vulnerable to cyberattacks, however good the system
  • Requires enhanced (expensive) maintenance

A real-world number worth remembering

  • Car engine piston installation: manual error rate ~1.5%; with automated systems, error rate falls to 0.00001% — a genuinely dramatic quality improvement from automation.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — automated system fundamentals and the nuclear/paracetamol examples.

Short answer 1
What is a DCS, and what is its main job in a nuclear power station?
Reveal answer

A Distributed Control System — a powerful computer monitoring and controlling the entire power station process, with no human interaction required, sending signals to actuators (valves, pumps, shutdown systems) when needed.

Short answer 2
Why does the supervisor of a nuclear power station DCS still matter, even though the process is "fully automated"?
Reveal answer

Because it's always possible for a set of conditions to occur that were never considered during testing, which could have safety implications — the human supervisor can OVERRIDE the DCS and shut the process down manually if something genuinely unexpected happens.

Fill in the blank 3
In an automated system, sensor data (if analogue) is first converted using an ______, processed by a ______, and the resulting output typically signals ______ to physically act.
Reveal answer

Blank 1: ADC. Blank 2: microprocessor/computer. Blank 3: actuators.

Spot the error 4
A student claims: "Since an automated system is fully automatic, it can never be vulnerable to a cyberattack." What's wrong with this?
Reveal answer

The lesson explicitly states any computerised system is subject to cyberattacks, no matter how good it is — full automation does NOT remove this risk; it's listed as a genuine disadvantage of both the nuclear power station and paracetamol examples.

Applied / scenario 5
A factory currently has workers manually monitoring a hazardous chemical process 24 hours a day. Using this lesson's content, explain two genuine benefits of replacing this with an automated system.
Reveal answer

Any two of: it's much SAFER (keeps humans out of the dangerous environment, and reacts faster/more reliably than a human); it's CHEAPER long-term (no need for round-the-clock human monitoring shifts); the process runs closer to OPTIMUM conditions since small deviations are caught and corrected very quickly.

Short answer 6
By roughly how much did automation reduce the piston-installation error rate in car engine manufacturing?
Reveal answer

From about 1.5% (manual) down to 0.00001% (automated) — a dramatic improvement.
