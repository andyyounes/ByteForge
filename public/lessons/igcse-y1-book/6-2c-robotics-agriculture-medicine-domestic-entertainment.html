6.2c Robotics: Agriculture, Medicine, Domestic & Entertainment
From weed-pulling vineyard robots to blood-drawing hospital robots to your own vacuum cleaner.
Theory

Agriculture

  • Harvesting/picking: robots (e.g. "vegebot", Cambridge University) use cameras to check ripeness before cutting — more accurate and faster than manual picking, higher yields, less waste.
  • Weed control: robots (e.g. used in French vineyards) use AI to distinguish weeds from crops, GPS tracking to navigate rows, and an actuator-driven blade — often guided by an aerial drone survey first.
  • Phenotyping (plant health/growth): spectral sensors + thermal cameras build a 3D plant model; machine learning spots leaf issues (blight, wrong colour) faster/more accurately than manual checks.
  • Seed-planting/fertiliser drones: aerial imaging for precise seed-planting and efficient, low-waste fertiliser spreading; drones can also do "cloud seeding" (adding silver iodide to force rainfall).
  • Many of these devices are genuinely AUTONOMOUS — using sensors/cameras to navigate around obstacles, or even programmed to "sleep" in bad weather.

Medicine

  • Surgical robots: safer, quicker, less costly operations — used for monitoring patients through to minor surgery.
  • Autonomous disinfecting of rooms/operating theatres.
  • Blood-sample-taking robots: less painful (better at finding a "good vein"), safer for staff around infectious patients, freeing staff for more skilled work.
  • Microbots: microscopic mechanical components (with a microprocessor) for TARGETED drug/therapy delivery to a specific site, reducing damage to surrounding tissue.
  • Prosthetic limbs: genuinely mini robots in their own right (meeting all 3 robot characteristics) — bionic skin/neural implants interface with the nervous system, using sensors/actuators for human-like responses (e.g. grip).

Domestic (home) robots

  • Autonomous vacuum cleaners: proximity sensors + cameras avoid obstacles, cover a whole room automatically; a microprocessor controls overall operation (and lets the user program it); actuators drive the motors.
  • Autonomous mowers: same sensor/camera/microprocessor/actuator setup as vacuum cleaners.
  • Personal assistants (e.g. "Vector"): microprocessor-controlled, cloud-connected; understands voice commands (microphone), answers questions, uses an HD camera + computer vision to recognise faces and navigate (proximity sensors + actuators).

Entertainment

  • Theme parks: autonomous character robots interact with visitors, making experiences "more realistic".
  • Music festivals: robotic lighting/laser displays, visual effects synchronised to the music.
  • Film/TV camera work: robots keep cameras steady, auto-focus during motion (e.g. the movie Gravity used robot arms to simulate zero-gravity human movement).
  • Humanoid "stunt" robots (remote-controlled or pre-programmed) perform impossible-for-humans actions, combined with CGI/image capture for special effects.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — robotics in agriculture, medicine, domestic use, and entertainment.

Short answer 1
How do vineyard weed-control robots navigate along rows of vines?
Reveal answer

Using GPS tracking to stay on course, with an actuator-operated weed removal blade under the control of the robot's microprocessor (often guided by an initial aerial drone survey of the vineyard).

Short answer 2
Give two real reasons robots are used to take blood samples from patients, according to this lesson.
Reveal answer

Any two of: less painful (better at finding a good vein); safer for doctors/nurses around infectious patients; frees up staff for more skilled work.

Fill in the blank 3
______ is the process of observing a plant's physical characteristics to assess its health and growth, done using spectral sensors and thermal cameras.
Reveal answer

Phenotyping.

Spot the error 4
A student claims: "Prosthetic limbs are just mechanical devices, not real robots, since they're attached to a human." What's wrong with this?
Reveal answer

The lesson explicitly states modern prosthetic limbs genuinely meet all three characteristics defining a robot (sensing, movement, programmability) — using sensors and actuators to give human-like responses like grip, making them mini robots in their own right, regardless of being attached to a human body.

Applied / scenario 5
A domestic robot vacuum cleaner needs to clean an entire living room without bumping into furniture or falling down stairs. Using this lesson's content, describe which components let it do this.
Reveal answer

Proximity sensors and cameras detect obstacles/edges to avoid collisions and cover the whole room automatically; a microprocessor controls overall operation (and can be programmed by the user); actuators drive the motors controlling forward/backward and side-to-side movement.

Short answer 6
Name one real example of robots being used in the film industry, according to this lesson.
Reveal answer

Any of: robot-controlled cameras (steady, auto-focus); the movie Gravity's use of robot arms to simulate zero-gravity human movement; humanoid stunt robots performing impossible-for-human actions.
