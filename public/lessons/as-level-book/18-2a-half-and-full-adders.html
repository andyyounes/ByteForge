18.02 Half and Full Adders
Building binary addition out of logic gates.
Theory

The half adder

  • Adding two bits: 1+1 produces a SUM of 0 with a CARRY of 1 — this carry cannot be ignored when adding multi-bit numbers.
  • A half adder takes two input bits (A, B) and outputs a sum bit (S) and a carry bit (C).

0110

  • C's truth table matches the AND gate exactly. S's truth table matches the XOR gate exactly.
  • So one valid half adder = ONE AND gate (for C) + ONE XOR gate (for S), both fed inputs A and B.
  • Since manufacturers prefer NAND/NOR gates (Chapter 4), a half adder is often built ENTIRELY from NAND gates instead — a functionally equivalent, more manufacturable circuit.

Why a half adder isn't enough for multi-bit addition

  • Adding multi-bit binary numbers starts from the LEAST significant bits and works up — at every stage EXCEPT the first, the carry from the PREVIOUS stage must also be incorporated.
  • A half adder only accepts TWO inputs — it has no way to also take in an incoming carry bit.

The full adder — three inputs

  • Takes THREE inputs: A, B, and Cin (the carry from the previous stage) — and outputs S and Cout.

  • A convenient construction: TWO half adders plus ONE OR gate. The first half adder combines A and B; the second combines that result's sum with Cin; the OR gate combines the two carry outputs to give the final Cout.
  • As with the half adder, a full adder can also be built entirely from NAND gates for manufacturing.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — half adders and full adders (part of Section 18.02).

Short answer 1
Which single logic gate's truth table exactly matches the carry (C) output of a half adder?
Reveal answer

The AND gate.

Short answer 2
Why can't a half adder alone handle multi-bit binary addition?
Reveal answer

A half adder only accepts two inputs, but every stage after the first needs to also incorporate the carry bit from the PREVIOUS stage — a third input a half adder has no way to accept.

Fill in the blank 3
A full adder can be built from ______ half adders plus one ______ gate.
Reveal answer

Two; OR.

Spot the error 4
A student tries to add two 4-bit binary numbers using four separate half adders, one per bit position, with no other connections between them. What will go wrong?
Reveal answer

Each bit position (except the very first) needs the carry OUT from the previous position fed in as a carry IN — using isolated half adders with no carry propagation between them would produce an incorrect sum for any addition that generates a carry.

Applied / scenario 5
For a half adder with inputs A=1, B=1, what are the S and C outputs?
Reveal answer

S = 0, C = 1 (matching 1+1 = 0 with a carry of 1).

Short answer 6
Why do manufacturers prefer to build adders entirely out of NAND (or NOR) gates rather than a mix of AND/OR/XOR gates?
Reveal answer

As covered in Chapter 4, NAND and NOR gates are "universal" gates that can implement any other gate's logic, which simplifies manufacturing to producing just one type of gate rather than several different ones.
