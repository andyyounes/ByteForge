9.05 The Internet's Consequences for Copyright
Piracy isn't new — the Internet just changed its scale, dramatically.
Key Terms
Piracy
Illegal copying of copyrighted media, now typically via downloading or streaming.
Ripping
Converting a product (e.g. from a CD/DVD) into a computer file, as the first step toward illegal distribution.
DRM
Digital Rights Management — technology aiming to prevent unauthorised copying/ripping.
Theory

Copyright breaches existed long before the Internet

  • Tape cassette recorders let individuals record radio broadcasts, or copy a friend's vinyl record.
  • Unrestricted workplace photocopier access let people copy printed material.

What genuinely changed with the Internet

  • The cinema, broadcast and music industries sell products as CDs/DVDs/Blu-ray discs.
  • Illegal copying ("piracy") now happens via downloading or streaming data originally released on these optical media.
  • The KEY difference from the tape/photocopier era: illegal copying now happens on a MAJOR SCALE, seriously affecting creators' profitability — not just occasional individual copying.

Ripping and DRM — an ongoing arms race

  • To make an illegal copy available for downloading/streaming, the data must first be "ripped" — converted from the original product into a computer file.
  • Producers use Digital Rights Management (DRM) to counter this. Originally, DRM just prevented a CD from playing on a computer (only a CD player) — now it must specifically try to prevent RIPPING, using encryption or deliberately damaged sectors.
  • Crucially: these DRM techniques do NOT guarantee piracy prevention.

Peer-to-peer file sharing — the major mechanism, and its tension with privacy

  • Peer-to-peer (P2P) file sharing is the major mechanism for media piracy.
  • There are moves to force ISPs to MONITOR P2P usage and report it to interested parties.
  • This faces considerable resistance — such monitoring amounts to a genuine breach of PRIVACY, creating a real tension between copyright enforcement and privacy rights.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — the Internet's consequences for copyright (completes Section 9.05).

Short answer 1
Name two ways copyright was breached BEFORE the Internet became dominant.
Reveal answer

Recording radio broadcasts or copying a friend's vinyl record using a tape cassette recorder; photocopying printed material using a workplace photocopier.

Short answer 2
What is the KEY difference between pre-Internet and Internet-era piracy, according to this lesson?
Reveal answer

Scale — Internet-era illegal copying happens on a major scale, seriously affecting creators' profitability, unlike the smaller-scale individual copying of the tape/photocopier era.

Fill in the blank 3
Converting an original product into a storable computer file, as the first step toward illegal distribution, is called ______.
Reveal answer

Ripping.

Spot the error 4
A student says: "DRM technology has successfully solved the problem of piracy." What does this lesson say is wrong?
Reveal answer

DRM techniques (encryption, deliberately damaged sectors) do NOT guarantee the prevention of piracy — it's an ongoing, unresolved arms race, not a solved problem.

Applied / scenario 5
Why do proposals to make ISPs monitor peer-to-peer file sharing face "considerable resistance", according to this lesson?
Reveal answer

Such monitoring amounts to a breach of privacy — creating a genuine tension between enforcing copyright and protecting users' privacy.

Short answer 6
What was DRM originally used for, before it needed to prevent ripping specifically?
Reveal answer

Simply to make a CD playable on a CD player but NOT on a computer system.
