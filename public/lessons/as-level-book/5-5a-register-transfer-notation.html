5.05 Register Transfer Notation
A precise shorthand for describing exactly what moves where, inside the CPU.
Theory

The fetch stage, written in register transfer notation

  • Register transfer notation (RTN) describes operations involving registers.
MAR ← [PC] PC ← [PC] + 1 ; MDR ← [[MAR]] CIR ← [MDR]

Reading the notation, piece by piece

  • The format resembles ordinary variable assignment: the item on the LEFT of the arrow is the DESTINATION of the data (identified by its register abbreviation).
  • To the RIGHT of the arrow is the DEFINITION of that data.
  • SQUARE BRACKETS around a register abbreviation, like [PC], mean "the CONTENTS of that register" (possibly with an arithmetic operation applied) — not the register itself.
  • Two operations on the SAME line, separated by a SEMICOLON, means those two transfers happen SIMULTANEOUSLY.

The trickiest part — double brackets, [[MAR]]

  • The CONTENT of the MAR is itself an ADDRESS.
  • So [[MAR]] means: take the content of MAR (an address), then go to THAT address in memory, and take the content stored THERE — that's the value being transferred to the MDR.
  • Single brackets [MAR] would just mean "the address stored in MAR" — DOUBLE brackets are needed to say "the value found AT that address".

Line-by-line walkthrough of the fetch stage in RTN

  • MAR ← [PC]: the CONTENT of the PC (an address) is copied into the MAR.
  • PC ← [PC] + 1 ; MDR ← [[MAR]]: SIMULTANEOUSLY, the PC's content is incremented by 1, AND the value stored at the address held in the MAR is copied into the MDR.
  • CIR ← [MDR]: the content of the MDR (the fetched instruction) is copied into the CIR.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — register transfer notation (completes Section 5.05).

Short answer 1
In register transfer notation, what does putting a register abbreviation inside square brackets, like [PC], mean?
Reveal answer

It means "the CONTENT of that register" — not the register itself.

Short answer 2
What does a semicolon between two register transfer statements on the same line indicate?
Reveal answer

That both transfers happen SIMULTANEOUSLY, not one after the other.

Fill in the blank 3
[[MAR]] means: take the ______ of MAR (an address), then take the ______ stored at that address.
Reveal answer

Content; value (content).

Spot the error 4
A student writes MDR ← [MAR] (single brackets) to mean "copy the value from the address held in MAR into the MDR". What's actually wrong with this?
Reveal answer

Single brackets [MAR] just means "the address stored in MAR" — it would copy an ADDRESS into the MDR, not the value stored at that address. To get the value AT that address, DOUBLE brackets are required: MDR ← [[MAR]].

Applied / scenario 5
Write, in register transfer notation, the statement that copies the CONTENT of the MDR into the CIR.
Reveal answer

CIR ← [MDR]

Short answer 6
In the fetch-stage RTN, which two operations are written on the same line separated by a semicolon, and why does that make sense?
Reveal answer

PC ← [PC] + 1 and MDR ← [[MAR]] — this matches exactly what was described in Section 5.04: the PC increments AND the instruction is fetched into the MDR during the same clock cycle.
