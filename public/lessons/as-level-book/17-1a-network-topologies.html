17.01 Isolated Network Topologies
Point-to-point, bus, ring, mesh, and star — the physical shapes a network can take.
Theory

The five requirements for data communication

  • A SENDER, a RECEIVER, a TRANSMISSION MEDIUM, a MESSAGE, and a PROTOCOL. A message can only be sent if sender and receiver agree on a protocol.

Point-to-point and bus

  • POINT-TO-POINT: the simplest possible network — two end-systems connected by a single, DEDICATED link.
  • BUS: one shared link used by MULTIPLE end-systems — a "multi-point" connection. Crucially, there is NO direct connection between any pair of end-systems; they all share the same medium.

Ring and mesh

  • RING: each end-system has a point-to-point connection to its two NEIGHBOURS, forming a closed loop.
  • FULLY-CONNECTED MESH: every end-system has a point-to-point connection to EVERY other end-system — never realistic at scale, because the amount of cabling required grows very fast.

Star — the dominant modern topology

  • Every end-system has a point-to-point connection to one CENTRAL device.
  • The star is the dominant configuration today, mainly because the central device can connect the whole network onward to OTHER networks, including the Internet.
  • With the right software, a star's central device can even make the network BEHAVE logically like a ring, while being physically wired as a star.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — network topologies (completes Section 17.01).

Short answer 1
List the five requirements for a data communications system.
Reveal answer

A sender, a receiver, a transmission medium, a message, and a protocol.

Short answer 2
Why is a fully-connected mesh topology never a realistic configuration for a large network?
Reveal answer

Because every end-system needs a direct link to every OTHER end-system — the amount of cabling required grows extremely fast as the number of end-systems increases.

Fill in the blank 3
In a bus topology, there is no direct connection between any pair of end-systems — they all share ______ link.
Reveal answer

One (a single).

Spot the error 4
A student claims a star topology has NO direct connections between end-systems, just like a bus. What's the misconception?
Reveal answer

In a star, each end-system DOES have a direct point-to-point connection — but to the CENTRAL device, not to other end-systems directly. This is different from a bus, where all end-systems share one link with no distinct central device.

Applied / scenario 5
Why is the star topology the dominant configuration for modern networks, rather than ring or mesh?
Reveal answer

Its central device provides a natural point to connect the whole network onward to OTHER networks (including the Internet) — something a ring or mesh doesn't naturally offer, and it avoids the excessive cabling a mesh would require.

Short answer 6
In a ring topology, how many other end-systems is each end-system directly connected to?
Reveal answer

Two — its two adjacent neighbours in the ring.
