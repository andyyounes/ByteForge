9.04 Ownership and Copyright
Copyright protects the WORK — never the idea behind it.
Key Terms
Copyright
A formal recognition of ownership of a created and published work.
Theory

Who owns the copyright?

  • If an individual creates AND publishes original work, that individual becomes the owner and can claim copyright.
  • Exception: if the individual works FOR an organisation, the organisation can claim copyright for work created by its employees.
  • Copyright CANNOT apply to an idea, and CANNOT apply to a component of a published work — only to the completed, published work itself.

What copyright can apply to

  • A literary work, a musical composition, a film, a music recording, a radio or TV broadcast, a work of art, or a computer program.

Why copyright exists — two justifications

  • Creation takes TIME, EFFORT, and original thinking — the creator should have the opportunity to be rewarded financially.
  • It's unfair for someone else to reproduce the work and profit from it without compensating the original creator.

Legislation — necessary, but not sufficient

  • Just like data protection (Chapter 8), legislation is needed to DETER copyright abuse — but cannot GUARANTEE no abuses occur.
  • Different countries have different legislative details, but there's international agreement that copyright can't be evaded simply by reproducing work in a different country from where it was created.
  • Typical copyright legislation includes: a REGISTRATION requirement recording the creation date; a DEFINED PERIOD for how long copyright applies; a policy for what happens if the copyright HOLDER dies; and an agreed method for indicating copyright (e.g. the © symbol).
A real copyright statement — the ACM's code of ethics

"This Code may be published without permission as long as it is not changed in any way and it carries the copyright notice. Copyright © 1999 by the Association for Computing Machinery, Inc. and the Institute for Electrical and Electronics Engineers, Inc."

What copyright means for actual USE of the work

  • The owner can include a statement about how the work may be used — the ACM example above is one such variation, specifically for work that hasn't been sold.
  • If someone has BOUGHT a copy of a copyrighted product, there's no restriction on making copies, PROVIDED they're solely for that individual's own use.
  • A common real example: a library user can photocopy PART of a book — a general regulation most copyright systems allow.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — ownership and copyright (completes Section 9.04).

Short answer 1
Can copyright apply to an IDEA? Explain.
Reveal answer

No — copyright cannot apply to an idea, only to a created and published WORK. It also cannot apply to a component of a published work.

Short answer 2
If an employee creates a published work as part of their job, who can typically claim copyright?
Reveal answer

The organisation the individual works for (not the individual employee).

Fill in the blank 3
Legislation can ______ copyright abuse but cannot ______ that no abuses occur.
Reveal answer

Deter; guarantee.

Spot the error 4
A student says: "If I move to a different country, I can freely republish a copyrighted work created elsewhere, since that country's laws don't apply to the original creator." What's wrong?
Reveal answer

There is international agreement that copyright laws cannot be evaded by reproducing a work in a different country from where it was created — this loophole genuinely doesn't work.

Applied / scenario 5
A person buys a copy of a copyrighted computer program. Are they allowed to make ANY copies of it? Under what condition?
Reveal answer

Yes — there's no restriction on making copies, provided they are solely for that individual's own use (not for distribution or resale).

Short answer 6
Give the two justifications for copyright's existence.
Reveal answer

(1) Creation requires time, effort and original thinking, deserving a chance at financial reward. (2) It's unfair for someone else to reproduce and profit from the work without compensating the original creator.
