23.02 Bubble Sort and Insertion Sort
Two standard sorting algorithms — comparing adjacent items vs. building up a sorted section one item at a time.
Theory

Bubble sort — a quick recap

  • Repeatedly steps through the list, comparing ADJACENT items and swapping them if they're in the wrong order — larger values gradually "bubble up" toward the end.
  • An efficient version stops early once a full pass makes NO swaps (the list is already sorted), and can also shrink the range checked each pass, since the largest remaining items settle into place at the end first.

Insertion sort — the card-sorting analogy

  • Imagine sorting a hand of cards: you hold a SORTED section in your hand, and take cards ONE AT A TIME from an unsorted pile, inserting each into its correct position among the cards you're already holding.
  • Compare the card to be inserted against the CURRENT card (starting from the end of the sorted section, working backward): if the new card is bigger, insert it below; otherwise, move to the next card up and repeat.
  • If you reach the top of the sorted section without finding a smaller card, the new card goes right at the TOP.
Insertion sort: a sorting algorithm that builds a sorted section of the list one item at a time, inserting each new item into its correct position.

Insertion sort in pseudocode

FOR Pointer ← 2 TO NumberOfItems ItemToBeInserted ← List[Pointer] CurrentItem ← Pointer - 1 // last item in the sorted part WHILE (List[CurrentItem] > ItemToBeInserted) AND (CurrentItem > 0) List[CurrentItem + 1] ← List[CurrentItem] // shift item down CurrentItem ← CurrentItem - 1 // look at the item above ENDWHILE List[CurrentItem + 1] ← ItemToBeInserted // insert item ENDFOR
  • The FOR loop starts at Pointer=2 (position 1 is trivially already "sorted" on its own).
  • The WHILE loop shifts sorted items UP one position at a time, making room, until it finds the correct spot for the new item — or runs out of items to compare against (CurrentItem reaches 0).
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — bubble sort and insertion sort (part of Section 23.02).

Short answer 1
What is the fundamental difference in approach between bubble sort and insertion sort?
Reveal answer

Bubble sort repeatedly compares and swaps ADJACENT pairs across the whole list. Insertion sort maintains a growing SORTED section and inserts each new item directly into its correct position within that section.

Short answer 2
In the insertion sort pseudocode, why does the FOR loop start at Pointer = 2, not Pointer = 1?
Reveal answer

A single item (position 1) is trivially already "sorted" on its own — there's nothing to compare it against yet, so sorting genuinely begins from the second item.

Fill in the blank 3
In insertion sort, the WHILE loop stops shifting items when it finds an item that is ______ than or equal to the item being inserted, or when it runs out of items to compare (CurrentItem reaches 0).
Reveal answer

Smaller.

Spot the error 4
A student implements insertion sort but forgets to store ItemToBeInserted in a separate variable before the WHILE loop starts shifting items. What goes wrong?
Reveal answer

The shifting step (List[CurrentItem+1] ← List[CurrentItem]) would overwrite the original value at List[Pointer] before it's been saved, permanently losing the item that was supposed to be inserted.

Applied / scenario 5
Dry-run insertion sort on the list [5, 2, 4] by hand. What does the list look like after processing Pointer=2 (inserting the value 2)?
Reveal answer

[2, 5, 4] — the 2 is compared against 5, found smaller, shifts 5 down one place, then 2 is inserted at position 1 (CurrentItem reaches 0, the loop condition fails).

Short answer 6
What real-world sorting activity does insertion sort closely resemble?
Reveal answer

Sorting a hand of playing cards one card at a time, inserting each new card into its correct position among cards already held in order.
