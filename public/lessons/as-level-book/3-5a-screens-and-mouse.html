3.05 Screens and Associated Technologies
From CRT to OLED, and the mouse that made the GUI usable in the first place.
Key Terms
Sub-pixel
One of three components (red, green, blue) making up a single screen pixel.
LCD
Screen back-lit by LEDs, with liquid crystal cells sandwiched between polarisers.
Resistive touch screen
A flexible surface causing contact between electrically resistive layers beneath, when touched.
Capacitive touch screen
A rigid surface above a conductive layer that undergoes a change in electrical state when touched.
Theory

The computer mouse — how it actually senses movement

  • Crucial point: a mouse has NO knowledge of absolute position. All it can do is register RELATIVE movement, which then influences the screen cursor's position.
  • Original mechanical mouse: a rubber ball in contact with two perpendicular rollers. As the ball rotates, each roller (attached to a spindle with a holed disc) causes intermittent light transmission through the holes — detected and converted into cursor movement data.
  • The optical mouse dispensed with the moving ball entirely — an LED shines light onto the surface, reflected back to a sensor. The sensor acts like a tiny camera, taking successive images; image-processing software compares them to work out the movement.

Screen pixels — three sub-pixels make one pixel

  • A screen pixel consists of THREE sub-pixels — one each for red, green, and blue. Varying the light level from each sub-pixel produces the full range of displayable colours.

The key distinction: does the pixel emit light, or just control it?

  • CRT and plasma: the pixel component itself IS a light source (phosphor, excited by an electron beam or by plasma discharge respectively).
  • LCD: the pixel does NOT emit light. It's illuminated by back-lighting, and each pixel affects how much of that light is transmitted through to the viewer, using a voltage applied to liquid crystal molecules to change their alignment (and so the light's polarisation).
  • OLED: back to being a direct light source again, but with no back-lighting layer required at all — a genuinely different construction from both CRT/plasma and LCD.

Touch screens — two different physical mechanisms

  • Resistive: has TWO layers separated by a thin gap beneath a NON-rigid screen surface. Pressing the screen physically moves the top layer into contact with the lower layer, creating a voltage divider that reveals the touch position.
  • Capacitive: uses a RIGID glass screen. A finger touching it causes a CAPACITANCE CHANGE in a circuit component immediately beneath the screen — no physical deformation of the screen is needed.
  • The most effective capacitive approach — projective capacitive touch (PCT) with mutual capacitance — uses an array of capacitors beneath the screen, enabling MULTI-TOUCH functionality (more than just a single point).
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — screens, the mouse, and touch screens (completes Section 3.05).

Short answer 1
What is the crucial limitation of how a computer mouse senses its own position?
Reveal answer

It has no knowledge of absolute position — it can only register relative MOVEMENT, which is then used to influence the screen cursor's position.

Short answer 2
How does an optical mouse detect movement, without any moving ball?
Reveal answer

An LED shines light onto the surface below; a sensor captures successive reflected images (like a tiny camera), and image-processing software compares them to work out the movement that has occurred.

Fill in the blank 3
Unlike CRT, plasma, and OLED, an ______ screen's pixels do NOT emit light themselves — they rely on ______ shining through them.
Reveal answer

LCD; back-lighting.

Spot the error 4
A student says: "Resistive and capacitive touch screens both work the same way — by physically pressing two layers together." Correct this.
Reveal answer

Only RESISTIVE touch screens work this way (a flexible surface presses two layers into contact). CAPACITIVE touch screens use a RIGID surface and detect a capacitance change caused by a finger's touch — no physical layer contact is involved.

Applied / scenario 5
A manufacturer wants a touch screen that supports genuine multi-touch gestures (like a two-finger pinch-to-zoom). Which touch technology described here is best suited, and why?
Reveal answer

Projective capacitive touch (PCT) with mutual capacitance — its array of capacitors beneath the screen specifically enables multi-touch functionality, unlike a simpler single-point resistive touch screen.

Short answer 6
What are the three sub-pixels that combine to make one full-colour screen pixel?
Reveal answer

Red, green, and blue.
