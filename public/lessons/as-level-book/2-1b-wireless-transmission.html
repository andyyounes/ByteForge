2.01 Wireless Transmission
Radio, microwave, infrared — one underlying phenomenon, three different frequency ranges. Completes Section 2.01.
Key Terms
Guided media
Transmission physically confined along a path — cables.
Unguided media
Transmission that spreads through open space — but only radio truly fits this fully.
Theory

Radio, microwave, infrared — all electromagnetic radiation

  • The ONLY intrinsic difference between radio, microwave, and infrared is the FREQUENCY of the waves.
  • Increasing frequency (radio → microwave → infrared) increases bandwidth, but DECREASES the ability to penetrate solid objects.
  • Interference is NOT consistently linked to frequency in the same simple way.

Why infrared is only suitable indoors

  • Infrared has the highest frequency of the three, and suffers the most attenuation.
  • It genuinely CANNOT penetrate a wall — but this is actually a BENEFIT indoors: the transmission can't escape the room and cause interference elsewhere.

Why microwave is usually the practical choice

  • For most real applications, microwave is the preferred option — its improved BANDWIDTH over radio is usually the deciding factor.

"Guided vs. unguided" is a slightly misleading label

  • Cables are often called "guided media"; wireless is often called "unguided media" — but this label only genuinely fits RADIO transmission.
  • Microwave and infrared CAN be directed toward a specific receiver, unlike a true "unguided" broadcast — so calling all wireless "unguided" oversimplifies things.

Real-world factors beyond pure technical performance

  • Specific wireless frequencies are REGULATED by government agencies — permission is required before use.
  • Outside regulated frequencies, no permission is needed to use the air for transmission — but laying a CABLE requires the landowner's permission wherever it's buried.
  • For global communication, fibre-optic cables (underground/seabed) and satellite transmission are competing technologies — currently neither dominates.
  • Interference is generally MORE significant for wireless than cable, and depends heavily on which frequencies are in use nearby.
  • Wireless generally needs repeaters LESS often than cable.
  • Mobile (cell) phones now dominate Internet use, and for these, wireless is the ONLY option.
  • For home/small-office use, wired and wireless are roughly equally efficient — the lack of a cabling requirement is the main factor favouring wireless for a small network.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — wireless transmission (completes Section 2.01).

Short answer 1
What is the ONE intrinsic difference between radio, microwave, and infrared transmission?
Reveal answer

The frequency of the waves — they are all forms of electromagnetic radiation, differing only in frequency.

Short answer 2
Why is infrared's inability to penetrate a wall actually an ADVANTAGE for indoor use?
Reveal answer

It means the transmission stays contained within the room and can't escape to cause interference with other devices/rooms elsewhere.

Fill in the blank 3
"Guided vs. unguided media" only genuinely fits ______ transmission, since microwave and infrared CAN be directed toward a receiver.
Reveal answer

Radio.

Spot the error 4
A student claims wireless transmission never needs permission from any authority, unlike cables (which need landowner permission). Why is this not entirely true?
Reveal answer

Specific wireless frequencies ARE regulated by government agencies, and permission is required to use them — it's only OUTSIDE these regulated frequencies that no permission is needed.

Applied / scenario 5
A company needs a wireless link between two office buildings on the same street, with good bandwidth, and doesn't want the signal to easily pass through the walls of unrelated buildings nearby. Which wireless option best fits, and why?
Reveal answer

Microwave — it offers improved bandwidth over radio (the usual deciding factor for most applications) and, being directional, can be focused specifically between the two buildings.

Short answer 6
For mobile (cell) phones, which transmission type is the ONLY option, and why?
Reveal answer

Wireless — a mobile phone by definition isn't physically connected to a cable, so it has no alternative to wireless transmission.
