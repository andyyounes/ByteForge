3.08 Input and Output of Sound
A microphone and a speaker are almost exact mirror images of each other. Completes Chapter 3.
Key Terms
Diaphragm
A flexible material that vibrates in response to sound (microphone) or creates sound by vibrating (speaker).
Condenser microphone
Uses a capacitance change to convert sound vibration into an electrical signal.
Theory

Sound input — the microphone

  • IP telephony and video conferencing are the two obvious use cases needing voice input; voice recognition is an alternative technique for data input generally.
  • A microphone has a DIAPHRAGM — a flexible material caused to vibrate by incoming sound. Connected to suitable circuitry, this vibration causes a change in an electrical signal.
  • A condenser microphone uses a CAPACITANCE change as its mechanism; an alternative uses a piezoelectric crystal.
  • The resulting electrical signal must be converted to a DIGITAL signal by an analogue-to-digital converter, before a sound (audio) card inside the computer can process it.

Sound output — the speaker (effectively the reverse process)

  • The sound card produces a DIGITAL signal, converted to analogue by a digital-to-analogue converter, then fed to the speaker.
  • Traditional technology: current flows through a COIL suspended within the magnetic field of a permanent magnet inside the speaker.
  • As the current's direction keeps reversing, the coil moves BACKWARDS and FORWARDS — this movement drives a diaphragm, which creates the actual sound.

Input and output are near-exact mirror images

  • Microphone (input): sound vibration → diaphragm moves → electrical signal → ADC → digital signal.
  • Speaker (output): digital signal → DAC → analogue signal → coil/diaphragm moves → sound.
  • Both rely on a diaphragm; both need a converter between the analogue and digital worlds — just running in opposite directions.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — sound input and output (completes Section 3.08 and Chapter 3).

Short answer 1
What physical component do BOTH a microphone and a speaker rely on?
Reveal answer

A diaphragm — a flexible material that vibrates (microphone: vibrates because of incoming sound; speaker: vibrates to create outgoing sound).

Short answer 2
What mechanism does a condenser microphone use to turn sound vibration into an electrical signal?
Reveal answer

A capacitance change.

Fill in the blank 3
Sound input needs an ______-to-digital converter; sound output needs a digital-to-______ converter.
Reveal answer

Analogue; analogue.

Spot the error 4
A student says: "A traditional speaker creates sound directly from the digital signal produced by the sound card." What's the missing step?
Reveal answer

The digital signal must first be converted to an ANALOGUE signal by a digital-to-analogue converter before it's fed to the speaker — the speaker itself works on the analogue signal, not the raw digital one.

Applied / scenario 5
In a traditional speaker, explain step by step how a changing electrical current ultimately produces sound.
Reveal answer

Current flows through a coil suspended in a permanent magnet's magnetic field. As the current's direction keeps reversing, the coil is pushed backwards and forwards. This coil movement drives a diaphragm, whose movement creates the sound.

Short answer 6
Name two obvious real-world applications that need voice INPUT to a computer system.
Reveal answer

IP telephony and video conferencing (voice recognition is also mentioned as an alternative data-input technique).
