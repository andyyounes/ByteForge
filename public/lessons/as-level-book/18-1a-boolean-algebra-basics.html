18.01 Boolean Algebra Basics
A shorthand notation and a set of laws for manipulating logic expressions.
Theory

Notation: 1 + 1 = 1?

  • In Boolean algebra, 1 represents TRUE, 0 represents FALSE, and + represents OR. So "1+1=1" reads as "TRUE OR TRUE is TRUE" — NOT ordinary arithmetic.
  • AND is written as a dot: A.B (this book's convention). NOT A ("the inverse of A") is written Ā (a bar over A).

The Boolean algebra laws

  • Almost every law has an AND form and an OR form. Swap every 0↔1 and every AND↔OR, and a law in one form transforms into the OTHER form.
  • Some laws resemble ordinary algebra with AND as multiplication and OR as addition — so the terms "product" and "sum" are used for Boolean algebra too.

De Morgan's laws, in plain English

  • The inverse of a Boolean PRODUCT (AND) becomes the SUM (OR) of the inverses of the individual values.
  • The inverse of a Boolean SUM (OR) becomes the PRODUCT (AND) of the individual inverses.
  • Example: 0.A = 0 reads "FALSE AND A is FALSE" — transforming 0↔1 and AND↔OR gives 1+A = 1, "TRUE OR A is TRUE".

Worked example — simplifying A + A.B

  • The claim: A + A.B simplifies to A + B. To prove it, we must first make the expression MORE complicated — this is the "lateral thinking" step.
Start: A + A.B Absorption (OR form, in reverse): A = A + A.B, applied to the first A: A + A.B + Ā.B Rearrange using commutative/distributive: A + B.(A + Ā) Inverse identity: A + Ā = 1, so: A + B.1 Identity law: B.1 = B, so: A + B
  • Simplifying with Boolean algebra is NOT a routine you can just memorise — it usually needs this kind of lateral, "make it more complex first" thinking.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — Boolean algebra notation and laws (completes Section 18.01).

Short answer 1
In Boolean algebra, what do 1, 0, and + represent?
Reveal answer

1 represents TRUE, 0 represents FALSE, and + represents OR.

Short answer 2
State De Morgan's law for the inverse of a Boolean product, in plain English.
Reveal answer

The inverse of a Boolean product (AND) becomes the sum (OR) of the inverses of the individual values: (A.B)‾ = Ā + B̄.

Fill in the blank 3
To convert a law from its AND form to its OR form, swap every 0 with ______ and every AND with ______.
Reveal answer

1; OR.

Spot the error 4
A student reads the Boolean expression "1+1=1" and concludes Boolean algebra has broken ordinary arithmetic. What's the actual meaning?
Reveal answer

It's not arithmetic at all — 1+1=1 in Boolean algebra reads as "TRUE OR TRUE is TRUE", a logic statement, not a numeric sum. The symbols 1, 0, and + carry entirely different meanings here.

Applied / scenario 5
Using the OR form of the absorption law, simplify B + B.C.
Reveal answer

B (directly matching the absorption law's OR form, A + A.B = A, with A replaced by B and B replaced by C).

Short answer 6
Why does simplifying a Boolean expression sometimes require making it MORE complicated first?
Reveal answer

Because some simplification laws (like absorption) can be applied in REVERSE to introduce a new term that later cancels out via other laws (like the inverse identity) — the expression must temporarily grow before it can shrink to its simplest form.
