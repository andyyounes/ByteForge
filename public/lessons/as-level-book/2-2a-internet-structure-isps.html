2.02 The Internet: Structure & ISPs
The Internet isn't ONE network — it's the biggest internetwork of independent networks ever built.
Key Terms
Internetwork
Multiple independent networks connected together — what the Internet actually is.
ISP
Internet Service Provider — gives an individual or organisation access to the Internet.
IXP
Internet Exchange Point — where connections between different ISPs are handled.
PSTN
Public Switched Telephone Network — the traditional phone system ("POTS"), now upgraded to fibre-optic/digital.
Theory

The Internet is NOT a WAN — a genuinely important distinction

  • WANs (Wide Area Networks) emerged in the 1970s (ARPANET being the classic example). LANs (Local Area Networks) followed in the 1980s with the arrival of the PC, then MANs (Metropolitan Area Networks) came later.
  • The Internet is the biggest INTERNETWORK in existence — a network of independent networks connected together — not a single WAN.
  • Crucially, the Internet was NEVER designed as one coherent entity — it simply evolved into its current form, and is still evolving. This is exactly why there's no single agreed definition of its exact structure.

The (informal) hierarchy of ISPs

  • Access ISP: gives an individual or company actual Internet access — the original, most basic ISP function.
  • Middle-tier / regional ISPs: access ISPs often connect through these.
  • Tier 1 / "backbone" ISPs: the top of the hierarchy — connections BETWEEN ISPs are handled by Internet Exchange Points (IXPs). Major content providers share this top tier alongside Tier 1 ISPs.

PSTN, dial-up, and leased lines

  • PSTN (Public Switched Telephone Network, historically "POTS") originally carried only ANALOGUE voice data.
  • To send DIGITAL data over it, a MODEM converts digital → analogue for transmission, with another modem converting back at the receiving end.
  • Dial-up: modest-speed, SHARED access, only connected when needed.
  • Leased line: a DEDICATED link with guaranteed transmission speed, permanently connected — organisations typically used these to build their own MANs/WANs.
  • Modern PSTNs have upgraded their main lines to fibre-optic/digital technology — enabling better leased-line services AND letting phone companies become ISPs themselves, offering both broadband and WiFi hotspot connectivity.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — Internet structure and ISP hierarchy.

Short answer 1
Why is it wrong to call the Internet "a WAN"?
Reveal answer

The Internet is an INTERNETWORK — many independent networks connected together — not a single Wide Area Network.

Short answer 2
Why is there no single agreed definition of the Internet's exact structure?
Reveal answer

Because the Internet was never DESIGNED as one coherent entity — it evolved organically into its current form and is still evolving, so there was never a single blueprint defining a fixed structure.

Fill in the blank 3
A ______ line provides a dedicated, permanently connected link with guaranteed speed; ______ access is shared, modest-speed, and connects only when needed.
Reveal answer

Leased line; dial-up.

Spot the error 4
A student says a modem is needed because "digital data can't travel down a phone line at all". Why is this not quite accurate?
Reveal answer

A traditional PSTN line was originally designed to carry ANALOGUE voice signals — a modem's job is to convert digital data INTO an analogue signal for transmission (and back again at the other end), not because digital data is impossible on the line, but because the line was built for analogue.

Applied / scenario 5
Two ISPs need to exchange traffic with each other. What kind of facility handles this connection between them?
Reveal answer

An Internet Exchange Point (IXP).

Short answer 6
What TWO connectivity services can a PSTN-turned-ISP now offer, following its upgrade to fibre-optic/digital technology?
Reveal answer

A broadband network connection, and WiFi hotspot technology.
