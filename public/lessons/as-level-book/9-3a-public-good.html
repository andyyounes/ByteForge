9.03 The Public Good
A rocket, a Mars probe, and an NHS IT project — three genuinely expensive lessons in what happens without sound ethics.
Theory

Public good draws on BOTH definitions of ethics

  • The code's language references: the health/safety/welfare of the public, the public interest, the public good, and public concern — with NO further indication of exactly how these should be interpreted.
  • Considering public good requires drawing on ethics' SECOND definition (moral principles) as well as the third (professional rules of conduct).
IncidentCauseCost to the public
Ariane 5 rocket (1996)A 64-bit floating point number couldn't convert into a 16-bit integer — overflow crashed the program, then the rocket~$500 million, exploded 40 seconds after launch
NASA Mars Climate OrbiterOne engineering group used Imperial units instead of the required SI units~$125 million, failed to reach orbit
UK NHS National Programme for ITProject failed to ever produce a workable system (commissioned 2002, scrapped 2011)~£12 billion spent (vs. an initial estimate under £3 billion)

What these three examples have in common — and one key difference

  • All three illustrate genuine public interest in successful software engineering — correctly applying the code of ethics (specification, testing, project management) could plausibly have saved enormous sums.
  • For the NHS example specifically: the software engineers are NOT to blame — but the code's PROJECT MANAGEMENT clauses, correctly applied, would likely have prevented this kind of fiasco.
  • In all three, the public concern was solely about COST from a failed project — not about the ETHICS of the endeavour itself.
A different kind of public concern — the ethics of the endeavour itself

Unlike the cost-failure examples above, some concerns are about WHAT a system does, not whether it worked:

• Powerful companies pressuring smaller companies into using their products even when alternatives are more suitable/cheaper.
• Companies providing systems with no guaranteed security against unauthorised access.
• Organisations concealing information about a security breach.
• Private data being stored and made available to security services.
• Social media allowing abusive/subversive content to spread.
• Search engines returning results with no concern for content quality.

Why this is genuinely hard for an individual to navigate

  • There is NO consistent public attitude to concerns like these — different people, genuinely, disagree.
  • This makes it difficult for an individual software engineer to judge what counts as "public good" in any given case.
  • Even when an individual DOES judge that a company isn't acting in the public good, it's often difficult to exert any real influence — and some individuals who have taken action have seen their own lives severely affected as a result.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — the public good (completes Section 9.03).

Short answer 1
What specific coding error caused the Ariane 5 rocket to explode?
Reveal answer

A line of code tried to convert a 64-bit floating point number into a 16-bit integer — the resulting overflow crashed the program, and with it the rocket.

Short answer 2
What caused the NASA Mars Climate Orbiter to fail to reach orbit?
Reveal answer

One group of software engineers used the Imperial system of units, while the rest of the software used the SI system — the mismatch only caused a problem at the orbit-achieving calculation stage.

Fill in the blank 3
In the Ariane 5, Mars Orbiter, and NHS examples, the public concern was solely about ______ — not about the ______ of the endeavour itself.
Reveal answer

Cost; ethics.

Spot the error 4
A student says: "The NHS IT project failure was purely the fault of the software engineers who wrote bad code." What does this lesson say instead?
Reveal answer

The software engineers are explicitly NOT to blame — the failure is attributed to poor PROJECT MANAGEMENT, and the code of ethics' management-focused clauses, if correctly applied, would likely have prevented this fiasco.

Applied / scenario 5
Give an example (from this lesson) of a public-good concern that is about the ETHICS of an endeavour itself, rather than a cost failure.
Reveal answer

Any of: powerful companies pressuring smaller ones into using their products; unguaranteed security against unauthorised access; concealing a security breach; private data made available to security services; social media allowing abusive content; search engines ignoring content quality.

Short answer 6
Why is it genuinely difficult for an individual software engineer to act on a public-good judgement?
Reveal answer

There's no consistent public attitude on many of these issues, making the judgement itself hard — and even when a judgement is made, an individual often has little real influence, with some who've taken action seeing their own lives severely affected.
