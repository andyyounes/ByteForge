2.02 Satellite Communication
GEO, MEO, LEO — three altitudes, three genuinely different jobs. Completes Section 2.02.
Key Terms
GEO
Geostationary Earth Orbit — highest altitude, appears fixed over one point on the equator.
MEO
Medium Earth Orbit — includes GPS satellites.
LEO
Low Earth Orbit — works in large "constellations", supplements mobile networks.

Why so few GEO satellites can cover the whole planet

  • A GEO satellite orbits at exactly the right altitude/speed to stay FIXED relative to a point on the equator — this is why just THREE of them, positioned correctly, can achieve full global coverage.
  • MEO satellites (used for GPS) need ten for global coverage; LEO satellites need around fifty, working together in "constellations" — though in practice, several HUNDRED LEO satellites are currently in use.

The Van Allen belts — genuine no-go zones

  • The Van Allen belts (regions full of charged particles) are areas satellites must avoid — they sit between certain altitude bands, constraining where satellites can safely orbit.

The fundamental trade-off of using a satellite at all

  • Advantage: height above the ground lets a satellite connect components separated by much GREATER distances than purely ground-based infrastructure could manage.
  • Disadvantage: the greater transmission DISTANCE causes transmission delays, which can cause real problems for the underlying technology supporting network operation.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — GEO/MEO/LEO satellites (completes Section 2.02).

Short answer 1
Why can just 3 GEO satellites achieve full global coverage?
Reveal answer

A GEO satellite orbits at the specific altitude/speed that keeps it fixed relative to a point on the equator — positioned correctly, just three can jointly cover the whole planet.

Short answer 2
Which type of satellite is used for GPS?
Reveal answer

MEO (Medium Earth Orbit) satellites.

Fill in the blank 3
LEO satellites work in large groups called ______, and are used to supplement ______ networks.
Reveal answer

Constellations; mobile (cell) phone networks.

Spot the error 4
A student says using satellites has NO real downside compared to ground-based infrastructure, since they can cover such huge distances. What's the genuine trade-off being ignored?
Reveal answer

The much greater transmission distance to and from a satellite causes real transmission DELAYS — this can cause genuine problems for the technology relying on that network connection, even though the satellite's height enables connecting far-apart locations.

Applied / scenario 5
A telecom company wants to provide long-distance telephone communication with the fewest possible satellites. Which type would they choose, and how many would they need at minimum?
Reveal answer

GEO satellites — just 3 are needed for full global coverage, the fewest of the three types.

Short answer 6
What are the Van Allen belts, and why do they matter for satellite orbits?
Reveal answer

Regions full of charged particles — genuine "no-go" zones that satellites must be positioned to avoid when choosing their orbital altitude.
