9.01 Ethics
Three definitions of one word — and which one this whole chapter is actually about.
Key Terms
Ethics (rules of conduct)
The rules of conduct recognised in a particular profession or area of human life — this chapter's focus.
Moral principles
The guiding principles by which a person acts — the foundation underneath professional rules of conduct.
Theory

Three definitions of "ethics" — and which one matters here

  • Ethics is the field of moral science (can be set aside for this chapter's purposes).
  • Ethics are the moral principles by which any person is guided.
  • Ethics are the rules of conduct recognised in a particular profession or area of human life — THIS is the chapter's focus.
  • Crucially, rules of conduct (definition 3) must inevitably reflect, at least in part, the moral principles (definition 2) underneath them — the two aren't fully separable.

Four viewpoints on right and wrong

  • Moral principles concern right or wrong — often linked to the concept of VIRTUE.
  • Right and wrong can be considered from: philosophical, religious, legal, or pragmatic viewpoints.
  • Philosophical debate has continued for over 2000 years (Aristotle, Confucius, and many others).
  • Religions have sometimes incorporated existing philosophies, or introduced their own.
  • Laws SHOULD reflect right and wrong (though don't always perfectly).
  • Pragmatism can be defined as applying common sense.

What actually grounds professional rules of conduct

  • Religious beliefs must be considered in a working environment, but this chapter doesn't discuss them further as a category.
  • Legal issues clearly impact working practices, but are RARELY the primary focus of rules of conduct.
  • What remains as the real foundation: PHILOSOPHICAL views of right and wrong, and PRAGMATIC views of common sense — these form the frame of reference for the rest of this chapter.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — the definitions of ethics (completes Section 9.01).

Short answer 1
Which of the three definitions of ethics given is the specific focus of this chapter?
Reveal answer

Ethics as the rules of conduct recognised in a particular profession or area of human life.

Short answer 2
Name the four viewpoints from which right and wrong might be considered.
Reveal answer

Philosophical, religious, legal, and pragmatic.

Fill in the blank 3
Rules of conduct for a profession must inevitably reflect, at least in part, the ______ ______ that are the foundation of ethics' second definition.
Reveal answer

Moral principles.

Spot the error 4
A student says: "Legal issues are the PRIMARY foundation for professional rules of conduct — if it's legal, it's ethical." What does this lesson say is wrong with that?
Reveal answer

Legal issues clearly impact working practices, but they are RARELY the primary focus in rules of conduct. The real foundation is philosophical views of right and wrong combined with pragmatic common sense — not simply "whatever is legal".

Applied / scenario 5
A software engineering professional code of conduct requires "acting in the public interest" even in cases the law doesn't explicitly cover. Which TWO foundations (from this lesson) does that requirement most likely draw on?
Reveal answer

Philosophical views of right and wrong, and pragmatic common sense — the two things this lesson identifies as the real foundation for professional rules of conduct, beyond just law.

Short answer 6
How can pragmatism be defined, according to this lesson?
Reveal answer

Applying common sense.
