5.1 Cybercrime & Cyber Attacks
Hackers vs. crackers, five real-world cybercrimes, and what to do if you become a victim.

What computer security and cybercrime are

  • Computer Security — protection against theft or damage to computer hardware, software, and information.
  • Cybercrime — crime committed through a computer and network. In Pakistan, offenders who interrupt someone's privacy or harm their reputation can face 3-5 years in jail plus a heavy fine.
Theory — Hackers vs. Crackers

Hacker

  • Has in-depth knowledge of systems/networks/programs.
  • Uses skills to find and fix network loopholes.
  • Shares what they discover.
  • "Ethical" / "white hat" hackers are hired by organizations to PREVENT data theft.

Cracker

  • Gains UNAUTHORIZED access to systems.
  • Bypasses passwords/licenses, changes source code, breaches security.
  • Acts with negative intentions — for money, fame, or fun.
  • Can make a system unavailable/non-functional.
Theory — Five Real-World Cybercrimes
  • Hacking — stealing WiFi/email/social media passwords, attacking websites, stealing sensitive data, erasing data.
  • Credit/Debit Card Scam — scammers copy card data (e.g. via a hidden device on an ATM) and use the card number, PIN, and security code to commit fraud.
  • Phishing — gathering personal information using FAKE emails/websites, posing as a trusted individual to ask for sensitive data.
  • Clickjacking — tricking someone into clicking a disguised link (e.g. a fake "reward" button) that installs malware or leaks private information.
  • Cyber Bullying/Harassment — using electronic means to post rumors, threats, leak personal info, blackmail, or commit hate speech with intent to harm.

What to do if you become a victim

  • Report the incident to trusted people — parents and teachers first.
  • In Pakistan, the National Response Centre for Cyber Crimes (NR3C) handles complaints — via website form, or the 9911 helpline (available 24/7).
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what this subsection taught — hackers/crackers, cybercrimes, and victim response.

Short answer 1
What is the key difference between a hacker and a cracker, as described here?
Reveal answer

A hacker uses skills to find/overcome network loopholes and often helps improve security (ethical hacking). A cracker gains unauthorized access with NEGATIVE intentions — breaching security, bypassing licenses, or causing harm.

Short answer 2
What is phishing, and how does it typically work?
Reveal answer

Phishing is gathering personal information using false emails/websites — the perpetrator poses as a trusted individual and asks the target to provide sensitive data like passwords or banking details.

Fill in the blank 3
A hacker hired by an organization specifically to prevent data theft is called an ethical hacker, also known as a ______ hat hacker.
Reveal answer

white.

Spot the error 4
A student says clickjacking only affects adults who aren't careful online. What's wrong?
Reveal answer

Clickjacking usually targets children or novice internet users specifically, tricking them into clicking malware links or sharing private information.

Applied / scenario 5
A student receives a message pretending to be from their bank, asking them to "confirm" their account password by replying. What type of cybercrime is this, and what should they do?
Reveal answer

This is phishing. They should NOT reply with any sensitive information, and should report it to a trusted adult (parent/teacher) rather than engaging with it.

Short answer 6
In Pakistan, what helpline number can a cybercrime victim call for 24/7 help?
Reveal answer

9911 (via the National Response Centre for Cyber Crimes).
