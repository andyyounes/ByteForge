5.3 Authentication Mechanisms
Three ways a system proves you are who you say you are.

What authentication is

  • A hardware or software-based mechanism that forces users to prove their identity before accessing data on a device — ensures only the AUTHENTICATED user gets access.

Username and Password

  • A pair of keywords known only by the user, presented to authenticate.
  • The default authentication mechanism on the web today — but large-scale attacks have made it an increasingly weak mechanism on its own; additional methods are often needed.

Personal Identification Number (PIN)

  • A security code for verifying identity, similar to a password — must be kept secret.
  • Provides security when a credit/debit card is lost or stolen, since the PIN is required before money can be withdrawn or transferred.

Biometric Verification

  • Relies on a person's unique BIOLOGICAL characteristics, not something they know or carry.
  • The system captures data in real-time and compares it to existing data in a database — if they match, authentication is confirmed.
  • Most common: fingerprint scanning. Others: retinal scans, iris, facial, and voice recognition.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what this subsection taught — the three authentication mechanisms.

Short answer 1
Why has username/password alone become an "unacceptable" authentication mechanism on its own?
Reveal answer

Recent large-scale computer attacks have shown username/password alone isn't secure enough — additional authentication mechanisms are needed to fully authenticate a user.

Short answer 2
What makes biometric verification fundamentally different from a password or PIN?
Reveal answer

It relies on a person's unique BIOLOGICAL characteristics (fingerprint, iris, voice, etc.) rather than something they know (a password/PIN) or could share with someone else.

Fill in the blank 3
The most common form of biometric authentication is scanning a person's ______.
Reveal answer

fingerprint.

Spot the error 4
A student says a PIN and a password are completely different concepts with nothing in common. What's wrong?
Reveal answer

A PIN is described as similar to a password — both are secret codes the user must keep confidential to verify their identity.

Applied / scenario 5
A bank wants a way to confirm a customer's identity that can't be stolen or guessed the way a password can. Which authentication type should they add, and why?
Reveal answer

Biometric verification — it's based on the person's own biological characteristics, which (unlike a password) can't simply be guessed or written down and shared.

Short answer 6
How does a biometric system decide whether to confirm authentication?
Reveal answer

It captures the person's biometric data in real-time and compares it against data already stored in a database — if both samples match, authentication is confirmed.
