5.4 Professional Ethics, Intellectual Property & Plagiarism
Information accuracy, patents/copyright/trademarks, software piracy, and plagiarism.

Professional ethics & information accuracy

  • Professional ethics are the personal/corporate principles guiding behavior within a profession — computing professionals should act for the public good: be honest, avoid harm, respect privacy, and only access resources when authorized.
  • Information Accuracy — a measurement assuring information is correct and true; it must come from reputable sources, not malicious/unreliable ones (especially relevant for social media).
Theory — Intellectual Property Rights (Three Types)

Patent

Exclusive rights to make/use/sell an invention for a limited period (20 years in Pakistan). Must be novel, have inventive steps, and be usable in industry.

Copyright

Legal rights for the creator of artwork/literature/information — "the right of copying". Gives an economic incentive to create new work.

Trademark

Identifies a product/service and distinguishes it from others — can be a word, phrase, logo, or symbol (often marked "TM").

In Pakistan, the Intellectual Property Organization (IPO) regulates IP rights matters.

Software Piracy

  • The illegal use, copying, or distribution of copyrighted software — causes major revenue loss for developers, leaving less for R&D.
  • Copy-protection and online registration have NOT been able to fully stop it.
  • Risks for users of pirated software: it may stop working anytime, and users lose access to customer support, upgrades, documentation, and bug fixes.

Plagiarism

  • Presenting someone else's work or ideas as your own, without acknowledging the original author.
  • Academic honesty requires citing ideas/words borrowed from others — failing to do so can bring serious penalties.
  • Turnitin is one commonly used plagiarism-detection service.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what this subsection taught — ethics, IP rights, piracy, and plagiarism.

Short answer 1
What's the key difference between a patent and a copyright?
Reveal answer

A patent protects an INVENTION (a process, machine, or method) for a limited time (20 years in Pakistan). A copyright protects creative/informational works like artwork, literature, or writing — giving control over how that specific work is used.

Short answer 2
Why is software piracy harmful even to the people using the pirated software, not just the developers?
Reveal answer

Pirated software may stop working at any time, and users can't access customer support, upgrades, technical documentation, training, or bug fixes.

Fill in the blank 3
Presenting someone else's work or ideas as your own, without acknowledging the original author, is called ______.
Reveal answer

plagiarism.

Spot the error 4
A student says a trademark protects an invention's manufacturing process. What's wrong?
Reveal answer

A trademark identifies and distinguishes a PRODUCT or SERVICE (like a logo or brand name) — it's a patent that protects an invention or process, not a trademark.

Applied / scenario 5
A student copies several paragraphs from a website directly into their essay without citing the source. What is this, and what consequence might they face?
Reveal answer

This is plagiarism — presenting someone else's work as their own. Schools can apply serious penalties, and services like Turnitin are commonly used to detect it.

Short answer 6
Why must information used professionally come from reputable sources?
Reveal answer

To ensure information accuracy — using unreliable or malicious sources results in inaccurate information, which is especially a risk with content shared on social media.
