4.2 Transmission Media
Guided (wired) vs. unguided (wireless) media, and the three causes of transmission impairment.
Theory — Guided Media (Wired)
  • Twisted Pair Cable — two insulated copper wires twisted together to reduce interference.
    • UTP (Unshielded) — cheap, easy to install, used for telephones; vulnerable to external interference.
    • STP (Shielded) — has a special coating to block interference; used for fast-data-rate ethernet and telephone data/voice channels.
  • Coaxial Cable ("coax") — two parallel conductors, each separately insulated, inside an outer plastic cover. Widely used for cable TV and analog TV networks.
  • Fiber-Optic Cable — transmits data as LIGHT, reflecting through a glass/plastic core surrounded by cladding. Used for very high-speed, large-volume data transmission.
Theory — Unguided Media (Wireless)
  • Radio Waves — omnidirectional (antennas don't need to be aligned), easy to generate, penetrate buildings. Used by FM/AM radio, TV, cordless phones.
  • Microwaves — line-of-sight transmission (antennas MUST be aligned); distance depends on antenna height. Used for mobile towers and TV broadcast. Two types: Terrestrial (both antennas on earth) and Satellite (one antenna in orbit).
  • Infrared — uses infrared light (LED transmitter, photodiode receiver); line-of-sight, cannot penetrate walls. Used for short-range wireless links.
Theory — Transmission Impairments (Three Causes)

Attenuation

  • Loss of signal energy due to resistance in the medium — strength decreases with distance. Fixed with amplifiers. Measured in decibels.

Distortion

  • Change in the SHAPE of a signal — different frequency components of a composite signal travel at different speeds and arrive out of phase.

Noise

  • An unwanted signal that mixes with the original during transmission — types include induced, crosstalk, thermal, and impulse noise.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what this subsection taught — guided/unguided media and transmission impairments.

Short answer 1
Why can fiber-optic cable transmit much more data at higher speed than twisted pair or coaxial cable?
Reveal answer

It transmits data as light reflecting through a glass/plastic core, which is specifically designed for transmission of large volumes of data at very high speed.

Short answer 2
What is the key requirement for microwave transmission that radio waves don't have?
Reveal answer

Microwaves need line-of-sight — sending and receiving antennas must be properly aligned. Radio waves are omnidirectional and don't need aligned antennas.

Fill in the blank 3
______ is the loss of signal energy due to resistance in the transmission medium, fixed using amplifiers.
Reveal answer

Attenuation.

Spot the error 4
A student says UTP cable is more resistant to external interference than STP. What's wrong?
Reveal answer

It's the opposite — STP (Shielded Twisted Pair) has a special coating to block interference, making it MORE resistant than UTP, which is vulnerable to external interference.

Applied / scenario 5
A remote mountain station needs internet access but has no nearby towers to form a direct line-of-sight link. Which type of microwave transmission would help, and why?
Reveal answer

Satellite microwave transmission — it works at remote places using an antenna in orbit, rather than needing another earth-based station nearby.

Short answer 6
A composite signal's different frequency components arrive at the destination out of phase with each other. Which transmission impairment is this?
Reveal answer

Distortion.
