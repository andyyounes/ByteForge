10.3a Half & Full Adders
Extension: the actual logic circuits that add binary numbers — completes the entire IGCSE Year 2 book.
Key Terms
Half adder
A circuit adding 2 bits, producing a sum bit and a carry bit — but can't accept a carry IN.
Full adder
A circuit adding 2 bits PLUS a carry-in, built from two half adders and an OR gate.
Theory

Half adder — the simplest binary addition circuit

  • Binary addition is one of the most basic operations any computer performs.
  • A half adder adds 2 bits (A and B), producing TWO outputs: the sum bit (S) and the carry bit (C).
  • Example: 1 + 1 = 10 in binary (denary 2) — the '1' is the carry, the '0' is the sum.

Recognising the gates hidden in this table

  • The SUM column is EXACTLY the XOR truth table — S = A XOR B.
  • The CARRY column is EXACTLY the AND truth table — C = A AND B.
  • So a half adder is really just an XOR gate and an AND gate, both fed the same two inputs A and B, each producing one of the two outputs.

Why a half adder isn't enough for real numbers

  • A half adder can't accept a CARRY-IN from a previous column — it only handles exactly 2 bits with no incoming carry.
  • Adding real multi-bit numbers (e.g. a full 8-bit byte) means every column (except the very first) needs to handle THREE inputs: the two bits being added, PLUS whatever carry came from the column before it.

Full adder — two half adders, joined by an OR gate

  • A full adder has THREE inputs: A, B, and Cin (carry in) — and two outputs: S (sum) and Cout (carry out).
  • It's built from TWO half adders plus one OR gate: the first half adder combines A and B; its sum output then goes into a SECOND half adder along with Cin.
  • The final sum (S) comes from the second half adder's sum output. The final carry-out (Cout) comes from ORing together the carry outputs of BOTH half adders — a carry could genuinely come from either step.

Reading the full adder — a real 3-bit column addition

  • Consider adding two 5-bit numbers where a column has A=1, B=0, and a carry-in of 1 from the previous column: the sum for THIS column is 0, and a NEW carry of 1 is passed to the NEXT column.
  • This is exactly why chaining full adders together (one per bit position) lets a computer add numbers of ANY length — each column's full adder passes its Cout into the NEXT column's Cin.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — half and full adders (completes this entire book).

Short answer 1
What are the two outputs of a half adder, and which single gate produces each one?
Reveal answer

Sum (S) comes from an XOR gate; Carry (C) comes from an AND gate — both fed the same two inputs A and B.

Short answer 2
What is the key limitation of a half adder that a full adder solves?
Reveal answer

A half adder cannot accept a carry-IN from a previous column — it only handles exactly 2 bits. A full adder adds a third input (Cin), needed for every column except the very first when adding multi-bit numbers.

Fill in the blank 3
A full adder is built from ______ half adders and one ______ gate.
Reveal answer

TWO half adders and one OR gate.

Spot the error 4
A student tries to add an 8-bit byte using only half adders (no full adders) chained together. Why does this fail from the second bit position onward?
Reveal answer

Every column after the first needs to handle a carry-IN from the previous column — a half adder has no way to accept that third input at all, so any carry generated would simply be lost or ignored, producing a wrong total.

Applied / scenario 5
Using the half adder truth table, what are S and C when A=1 and B=1?
Reveal answer

S = 0, C = 1 — matching 1 + 1 = 10 in binary (the carry is the '1', the sum is the '0').

Short answer 6
Why is Cout produced by ORing the two half adders' carry outputs together, rather than picking just one of them?
Reveal answer

A carry could genuinely be generated by EITHER half adder (the first one combining A and B, or the second one combining that sum with Cin) — ORing them together correctly captures a carry-out happening from either source.
