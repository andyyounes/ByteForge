7.2b Structure Diagrams
Drawing top-down design as a hierarchy — an alarm app broken into sub-systems, level by level.
Theory

What a structure diagram shows

  • Structure diagrams show top-down design in DIAGRAMMATIC form.
  • They are HIERARCHICAL — each level gives a more detailed breakdown of the system above it.
  • Any sub-system can be further divided if needed, going deeper into the hierarchy.
Basic structure diagram: the top-level system splits into sub-systems, and Sub-system 1 splits further into Sub 1.1 and Sub 1.2.
The alarm app decomposed into "Set alarm", "Check time", and "Sound alarm" — each further sub-divided (e.g. "Set alarm" splits into "Reset/clear alarm", "Set time", "Turn alarm on/off").

Reading a structure diagram

  • The TOP box represents the whole system.
  • Boxes on the SAME level represent sub-systems that together make up the level above them.
  • Lines connect a sub-system to the smaller sub-systems it's further broken down into.
  • A box with no lines below it is a "leaf" — it performs a single action and needs no further breakdown.
Practice — Test Yourself

These questions only cover what THIS subsection taught — structure diagrams.

Short answer 1
What does a structure diagram actually show?
Reveal answer

It shows top-down design in diagrammatic form — a hierarchy where each level gives a more detailed breakdown of the system above it.

Short answer 2
In the alarm app example, name the three top-level sub-systems.
Reveal answer

Set alarm, Check time, and Sound alarm.

Fill in the blank 3
Structure diagrams are ______, meaning each level gives a more ______ breakdown of the level above it.
Reveal answer

Blank 1: hierarchical. Blank 2: detailed.

Spot the error 4
A student claims: "Every box in a structure diagram must be broken down further, no matter how simple it already is." What's wrong with this?
Reveal answer

A sub-system only needs to be broken down further IF it doesn't yet perform a single, simple action. Once a box represents one clear action (a "leaf" of the hierarchy), it doesn't need further division — the diagram doesn't force every box to keep splitting regardless.

Applied / scenario 5
Draw (in words) a structure diagram for a simple online shopping "Checkout" system, with top-level sub-systems for reviewing the basket, entering payment details, and confirming the order.
Reveal answer

Top box: "Checkout". Three sub-systems beneath it: "Review basket" (further split into e.g. "Show items" and "Apply discount code"), "Enter payment details" (split into e.g. "Enter card details" and "Validate payment"), and "Confirm order" (split into e.g. "Show order summary" and "Send confirmation email") — matching the same top-down, hierarchical pattern as the alarm app example.

Short answer 6
What connects a sub-system box to the smaller sub-systems beneath it in the diagram?
Reveal answer

Lines, showing which smaller sub-systems that particular sub-system is further broken down into.
